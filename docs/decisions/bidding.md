# Bidding and realtime

Part of the decisions in `docs/DESIGN.md` section 7. Rules: Bid transaction, realtime, limits, outbid emails, reserve.

- Bid rules (2026-11-02): the transaction judges live/ended/extension by the database clock (`clock_timestamp()` after the row lock), so several machines agree. Only live auctions take bids. Each bid has an idempotency key unique per bidder: a retry answers 200 `replayed: true`. Refusals carry a `code` (`NOT_FOUND`, `OWN_LISTING`, `NOT_LIVE`, `MAX_NOT_HIGHER` (replaced `ALREADY_HIGHEST` on 2026-11-12), `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, `TOO_LOW` with `minimumNextBidCents`, `BLOCKED`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `BUSY`). A bid is at most EUR 10,000,000 (`MAX_BID_CENTS`) so the next minimum fits 32 bits. A database failure is 404 or 503 `BUSY` (safe to retry with the same key), never a bare 500. A listing with a bid cannot be removed, and neither a listing nor an account with bids can be deleted (`Restrict`).
- Realtime (2026-11-02): Socket.IO gateway, WebSocket transport only (no sticky sessions needed), Redis adapter when `REDIS_URL` is set. Rooms `auction:<id>` (anyone may watch, max 20 per connection) and `user:<id>` (joined by the server from a verified token only). The browser gets a 5-minute HS256 token from `apps/web/app/api/realtime-token`. Events `bid:placed` and `auction:extended` are sent only after the transaction commits, never for a replay; a failed push never fails the bid. The client ignores older messages and refetches the page after every connect or bid-count gap.
- Bid notifications (2026-11-02): after commit, the outbid bidder gets `notice:outbid` plus a delayed email, and other bidders get `notice:extended` when the end moved. Nothing for a first bid, refusal or replay. Toasts last 5 seconds; the outbid toast links to the listing to raise the maximum (it had a confirmed "Bid €minimum" button until proxy bidding, 2026-11-13). The extension toast is skipped on that listing's own page.
- Limits and audit (2026-11-02): 10 bids a minute per user and 30 per address (`BID_LIMIT_USER`, `BID_LIMIT_IP`), refused attempts included, in a fixed window in Redis (in-memory without Redis). If Redis is down the attempt is allowed, because the database still decides. Every attempt writes a `BidAudit` row (accepted ones inside the bid transaction); malformed or signed-out requests are not recorded. A replay is answered before limits and does not count. A flood writes one `RATE_LIMITED` row per user per minute. The visitor's address is passed from the web server in `x-viewer-ip` with an HMAC signature (`AUTH_SECRET`); the API trusts it only if valid. `TRUST_PROXY` sets how many proxies Express believes. Audit rows are kept as long as bids.
- Outbid email timing (2026-11-02): emails are queued in `PendingOutbidEmail` and sent by a sweeper (every 30 s), due 3 minutes after the outbid (or at once if the auction ends sooner), so a bidding war collapses into one email or none. Skipped if the person is high bidder again or has the auction page open. At most one per person and auction per 15 minutes. A send leases its row and deletes it only after success, retrying up to 3 times, so a stopped machine delays and never loses an email.
- Reserve price: the amount is visible to everyone (2026-10-30). If not met at close there is no sale and the seller can relist.
- **Bidding on the listing page:** current bid or starting price, bid count, high bidder, minimum next bid, reserve with "(met)"/"(not met)" once there are bids, and the latest 5 bids (`BID_HISTORY_SHOWN`). The bid box ("Your maximum bid") starts at the minimum and refuses lower amounts client-side. Every bid needs a second click ("You cannot lower or withdraw it"), with an extra warning at ten times the minimum or more. The bid carries an idempotency key kept while the outcome is unknown. Signed-out visitors, the seller, blocked bidders, and upcoming/ended auctions see a note instead of the box; the high bidder sees their own maximum and a box to raise it (see "As built" below). The page updates live over WebSocket.
- **Notifications:** outbid email plus a live toast that links to the listing to raise the maximum. When a late bid extends an auction, viewers see the countdown jump and an amber banner (kept for the rest of the auction while `extensionCount` > 0), the outbid bidder gets the usual notice with the new end time, and other bidders signed in elsewhere get a toast. The extension that reaches the ceiling is "Final chance to bid" (toast, and a yellow tag on cards; no banner, removed by the owner). There is deliberately no email to all bidders, to keep email volume low.
- Proxy bidding (decided 2026-11-12, owner's request; it overturns "no proxy bidding in v1"; the design is in `docs/PROXY-BIDDING-PLAN.md` and the build log in `tasks/`, both kept for the record): the number a bidder types is a **maximum**. The leader's maximum is kept in `AutoBid` (one row per listing and bidder, only ever raised) and never sent to anyone but its owner. `Bid` stays the history: `kind` is `BID` (the row a request produced), `AUTO` (a row the system placed for the leader) or `RAISE` (a hidden raise of the leader's own maximum). The rules, with `P` the current price, `inc(p) = max(€1, 5% of p rounded up)`, `L` the leader with maximum `Ml` and `C` a challenger with maximum `M`:
  - First bid: the price is the starting price, whatever the maximum; `C` leads.
  - `C` is the leader: `M` must be above `Ml` (else 409 `MAX_NOT_HIGHER`); nothing visible changes.
  - `C` is someone else: `M` must still meet the minimum next bid (`TOO_LOW`). If `M > Ml`, `C` takes the lead at `min(M, Ml + inc(Ml))` after `L`'s counter at `Ml`. If `M = Ml`, the earlier maximum wins at `M` (D3). If `M < Ml`, `L` keeps the lead at `min(Ml, M + inc(M))` and `C` is told at once (`outcome: OUTBID_BY_PROXY`, a 201, not an error).
  - Reserve (D1, owner chose yes): if the leader's maximum is at least the reserve and the price is below it, the price rises to the reserve.
  - Anti-sniping (D2, owner chose yes): any request that changes the price in the last 2 minutes extends the auction as before, once per request however many rows it creates; a hidden raise never extends.
  - Ineligible leader (D8, owner chose yes): a leader who is banned, has asked to delete their account or has two strikes no longer defends; they count as a plain bid at the current price.
  - Outbid toast (D6, owner chose yes): it says someone's maximum is higher and links to the listing to raise it. There is no one-click bid.
  - Defaults taken as recommended (the owner may overrule any): D3 ties go to the earlier maximum; D4 the history does not label automatic rows; D5 a maximum cannot be lowered or withdrawn; D7 the first bid shows the starting price; D9 `bidCount` counts every visible row (`BID` and `AUTO`); D10 the 10x warning and the second click stay, worded for a maximum; D11 the wire field stays `amountCents` (it now means the maximum) so the API can deploy before the web; D12 rate limits are unchanged; D13 admins do not see maximums; D14 a runner-up after an unpaid win is offered their highest visible bid.
- Proxy bidding, as built (2026-11-13; it follows the rules above, and these are the parts the rules do not cover):
  - Events and the page: `bid:placed` carries `bids` (every visible row the request made, oldest first) next to `bid` (the row that set the price, kept for older clients); a hidden raise sends an empty `bids`. The live store adds all the rows and asks the server for the page only when rows are missing (`missedBids` compares `bidCount` with the rows in the event), so a defended bid (two rows) no longer refreshes the page. A bid response adds `outcome`, `yourMaxCents`, `rows` and `requestedBy`; an older API without `outcome` is read as leading.
  - Nobody else sees a maximum: it is in no socket event, listing, list, history or other bidder's response. `GET /listings/:id` adds `viewerMaxCents` for a signed-in viewer who has one and for nobody else. `realtime.e2e.test.ts` bids with a distinctive maximum and checks every payload a visitor, another bidder and the seller can get, and fails if one is added (checked by adding it three ways). Not hidden, because it is inherent: the price is at most one step above the second-highest maximum.
  - The bid panel: the first bid says what it is ("We bid for you, one step at a time, up to this amount, so you only pay what is needed to stay ahead"). After a bid one line says what it meant: in front at the price, "Someone has a higher maximum. The price is now €X" with a "Raise your maximum" button, or "Your maximum is now €Y". A line that quotes a price is dropped once the live price is above it (prices only rise, so it is kept until then, including while the page catches up). The person in front sees "Your maximum: €X" and a raise box that starts one step above it and refuses anything not above it, before and after sending.
  - Notices: the outbid toast, bell entry and email say someone's maximum is higher and, in the email, to set a new maximum of at least the minimum. Bidding again clears the requester's own waiting outbid email (not the leader's), and the extension toast skips the requester as well as the leader and the outbid bidder. A defended bid outbids nobody, so nobody is told they were outbid.
  - Other readers: the runner-up after an unpaid win, the public history and the admin bid counts and "Auctions they bid on" ignore `RAISE` rows. The bot bidder takes `--proxy` (a maximum between the minimum and five times it, so some bids are defended); it uses the seed's four-digit user names, which it had stopped matching.
