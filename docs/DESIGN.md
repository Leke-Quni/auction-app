# Auction App: Design & Decisions

Status: design agreed with the owner in a grilling session on 2026-10-30. No code written yet. This file is the source of truth. If you need to deviate from it, ask the owner first and update this file.

## 1. Product

- Portfolio/learning project built to production-quality standards. It is not expected to launch, so there is no legal, tax, or compliance work. Why: the owner doesn't expect it to go live.
- Multi-seller marketplace: one account type, every user can buy and sell.
- Reference countries: Kosovo, Albania, North Macedonia. Stripe availability there is unverified (third-party lists show Albania and North Macedonia for Connect payouts, Kosovo unclear; official list not checked). Why this matters: payment and shipping providers sit behind interfaces so they can be swapped.
- Currency: EUR only. Language: English only. Prices are stored as integer cents. All timestamps are UTC, displayed in the user's timezone. `next-intl` and ALL/MKD can be added later.

## 2. Auction rules

- Timed English auctions with an optional reserve price.
- Durations: 1, 3, 5, 7, or 30 days (1 month; added 2026-10-30).
- Bids are manual and binding. No proxy/max bidding in v1 (it complicates bid logic a lot).
- Minimum next bid = `current + max(€1, 5% of current)`, with the 5% rounded up to a whole cent (cent precision). The user types any amount at or above it. The UI shows the minimum as a hint and the API enforces it. The first bid only needs to meet the starting price. The 5% is a config value. The rule lives in `packages/shared` so web and API apply the same logic. Why: the owner rejected fixed tiers and wanted free-entry bids, but not +1 cent nudges.
  - Examples: current €10 → min €11; €100 → €105; €1,000 → €1,050.
- Anti-sniping (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4; replaced the earlier two-step rule the same day): a bid in the last 2 minutes sets the end time to 2 minutes after that bid, every time it happens, so a bidding war keeps going while people answer each other. A bid earlier than that leaves the end time alone. The only limit is a ceiling: the end time can never pass 24 hours after the auction's original end (start plus duration), so every auction still finishes.
- Sellers cannot bid on their own items (shill-bidding is the main fraud risk).
- Bids are rate-limited (per user and per IP, in Redis) and audit-logged with user ID and IP.
- Bidding requires a verified email only (no saved payment method).

## 3. Close and payment

- QStash schedules an idempotent close job per auction (rescheduled on extension), plus a periodic sweep as a safety net. Idempotency prevents a duplicate delivery from closing or charging twice.
- The winner gets an email and a pay-now page with 48 hours to pay by card. Why: the owner wants payment sorted after the auction ends, not a pre-authorization (card holds expire in about 7 days). Before paying, the buyer enters a delivery address and picks one of our delivery options; the card payment is the item price plus the delivery price (added 2026-11-04, step 4b). Our carrier collects from the seller's pickup address, so the seller does not post anything.
- If the window expires: the item is offered to the second-highest bidder, and the non-payer gets a strike. Two strikes blocks bidding. The seller can relist with one click.
- The platform takes a configurable percentage of the item price from the seller (5%, capped at EUR 50 per sale; changed 2026-11-04), with no listing fees or buyer premium. Delivery money is not part of the fee.
- `PaymentProvider` interface (checkout session create/confirm, refund, payout): Stripe test mode as the main implementation, in-memory fake for tests.
- `ShippingProvider` interface: fake provider that moves orders `label created → in transit → delivered` on a timer. The seller marks the item shipped. Payout is released after delivery or buyer confirmation. Why: no real carrier integration, since the project won't go live. Delivery prices are not quoted by it: they are ours (see step 4b in section 7), and no carrier is integrated.

## 4. Features

- Listings with up to 8 images (under 5 MB each), uploaded straight from the browser to Vercel Blob via signed tokens, so large files never pass through our server.
- Search: Typesense (self-hosted, see section 7) with typo tolerance, filters (category, condition, price range, ending soon), and sorting (ending soonest, newest, most bids, price). Possible later: geo search, autocomplete, semantic search.
- Fixed list of about 10 seeded categories.
- Search page (added 2026-11-02, slice 3): `GET /search` (API) and `/search` (web). Filters: category, condition, a price range (in euros on the page, cents in the API), and "ending within 24 hours". Sorts: best match, ending soonest, newest, most bids, price both ways; with text the default is best match, without it newest first. Typo tolerance and partial words come from Typesense: 1 typo from 4 letters, 2 from 6 (its maximum), and three or more of the same letter in a row are cut to two before searching. Synonyms (added 2026-11-02) are listed in `apps/api/src/search/synonyms.ts` and pushed to Typesense when the API starts: a general word such as "shoes" also finds sneakers, boots and similar, one way only, and spellings such as "watercolor" match "watercolour". This needs Typesense 30 or newer (its synonym sets API). Only live and upcoming auctions appear. The search page and the home page share a sidebar of categories and filters (price slider plus typed boxes, sort applies at once, the rest on Apply; both lead to /search); the header search shows the text, keeps the filters when searching again, and lists them as chips that each remove one filter. The header search is left out of the sign-in, sign-up and password pages. Searching with nothing typed and no filter chosen does nothing, so you stay on the page you are on (changed 2026-11-03). Recent searches (added 2026-11-03): the last 8 searches made in that browser are kept in `localStorage` (`apps/web/lib/recent-searches.ts`, nothing stored on the server) and listed under the header search box while it is focused and empty, each with a × to forget it and a Clear link; a repeat moves to the top, whatever its letter case. Suggestions as you type (built 2026-11-03): once at least 2 letters are typed, the box asks `GET /search/suggest?q=` (through the web app's `/api/suggest`, 200 ms after typing stops) and lists up to 5 titles of live and upcoming listings, best match first and each title once, under "Suggestions"; choosing one searches for it and remembers it. It asks the same `SearchIndex.search` as the search page, so it gets typo tolerance and synonyms with Typesense and partial words from the Postgres fallback. Any failure shows no suggestions and never an error. The up and down arrow keys move a highlight through whichever list is showing (recent searches or suggestions, wrapping round; ArrowDown also opens a closed list), and Enter searches the highlighted one, or the typed text when nothing is highlighted (added 2026-11-03). Both lists use light grey text on white. It has no rate limit of its own yet. Search is unavailable (503) when Typesense is down.
- Bidding on the listing page (added 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 3): the page shows the current bid (or the starting price), the number of bids, the high bidder, the minimum next bid, the reserve with "(met)" or "(not met)" once there are bids, a bid history of the latest 5 bids (highest first, your own marked; the footer says "Showing the highest 5 of N bids" when there are more; changed from 20 on 2026-11-03, `BID_HISTORY_SHOWN` in `packages/shared`). The bid box starts at the minimum and says it; a bid below it is refused before it is sent; every bid needs a second click: "Place bid" shows "You are about to bid €X. A bid cannot be withdrawn." with "Confirm bid of €X" and "Change" (added 2026-11-02, so nobody bids by accident), and a bid ten times the minimum or more adds a warning to that step. The bid is sent with a key that is kept while the outcome is unknown, so a retry never places it twice. Signed-out visitors, the seller, the current high bidder, and upcoming or ended auctions see a short note instead of the box. Cards show "current bid" and the number of bids once there are bids. The page updates live for everyone over the WebSocket (step 4, built 2026-11-02): see the realtime decision in section 7.
- Main page (added 2026-11-02, slice 2): "Ending soon" (live auctions ending within 24 hours, soonest first) and "Upcoming" (not started yet, soonest start first) sections of 4 cards each sit above the full list on page 1. A section with no auctions is hidden. `GET /listings` takes an optional `section=endingSoon|upcoming` for this. Slice 3 search may replace it.
- Watchlist with "ending soon" emails.
- Seller profiles with post-sale ratings from the buyer.
- Users have a first name, last name, and username. The username is the public handle (header, later listings and bids); real names stay private. Usernames are 3-20 characters that may contain lowercase letters, numbers, and underscores (none required), unique, with a few reserved names (such as `admin`) blocked, and can be changed any time. Sign-in is by email, not username.
- Account settings (`/account/settings`): change first/last name, username, email, and password. Changing the email or password needs the current password, and an email change is confirmed with a 6-digit code sent to the new address. Every place a new password is entered (sign-up, change, reset) asks for it twice. Forgot password emails a link that expires after 1 hour and works once; using it also verifies the email and does not sign the user in.
- Outbid notifications: email, plus an in-app realtime toast ("You've been outbid") with a one-click bid action.
- Auction-extended notices (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4): when a late bid moves the end time, everyone viewing the listing sees it live (the countdown jumps and an amber banner shows the new end time; the banner stays on the listing page for the rest of the auction, after a reload too, since it follows `Listing.extensionCount` being above 0); the bidder who was just outbid gets their usual outbid email and toast, now with the new end time; every other bidder on that auction who is signed in elsewhere on the site gets an in-app toast. The extension that takes the end time to its ceiling is marked more strongly: the toast for other bidders says "Final chance to bid", and the yellow "Final chance to bid" tag on listing cards (which follows `finalChance`) stays for the rest of the auction. There is no banner for it on the listing page (removed by the owner on 2026-11-02; it had first been a red banner, then amber). There is deliberately no email to the top 10 or to all bidders (the owner's choice, to keep email volume low); each late bid can send these notices, but the 5-second toasts and the one-email-per-outbid rule keep the noise down, and the ceiling bounds how many there can be.
- Emails (Resend, sent via QStash so a slow provider never blocks a bid): outbid, won, pay-now, ending soon (watchlist), shipping updates.
- Report button on listings.
- Contacting the seller (added 2026-11-02, after slice 4; the owner moved messaging into scope, then dropped private messages on 2026-11-03 because public questions cover it): public Q&A on the listing page (a signed-in user asks, the seller answers, everyone sees it). There are no private threads and no inbox. A new question or answer sends an in-app notification (live toast and unread count over the WebSocket) and an email. It is built as slice 4b, after slice 4 and before slice 5. Details are open decisions in section 7.
- Admin app: moderation (remove listings, ban users) and reports queue.

Out of scope for v1: proxy bidding, buy-it-now, offers, private messages between buyer and seller (public questions cover it), push/SMS, nested categories, automated content moderation, real shipping carriers, multiple currencies or languages.

## 5. Architecture

Turborepo monorepo:

- `apps/web`: Next.js storefront (Vercel).
- `apps/admin`: separate Next.js app with its own theme (own Vercel project). Calls the same API through admin-only routes guarded by an `ADMIN` role in the API (never trust the UI). No public sign-up.
- `apps/api`: NestJS modular monolith (auctions, bidding, payments, users, notifications) with in-process domain events (`BidPlaced`, `AuctionClosed`, `AuctionExtended`) and a transactional outbox delivered through QStash. Modules talk through interfaces and events, not by reaching into each other's tables. Hosted on a long-running container host for native WebSockets with the Redis adapter. Why: Vercel functions cannot hold persistent WebSockets.
- `packages/db`: Prisma schema and client. Only this package imports Prisma.
- `packages/shared`: types, Zod schemas, bid rules.
- `packages/ui`: shared unstyled primitives (Tailwind + shadcn/ui). Each app has its own theme tokens (storefront consumer-friendly, admin dense and table-heavy).
- `packages/config`: shared tsconfig and eslint.

Data flow and rules:

- Next.js apps get all data through the API and never touch Postgres directly.
- Bid path: **Postgres decides** (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, replacing the earlier plan of a Redis Lua script plus a reconciliation job). A bid runs in one database transaction that locks the listing row, checks the minimum, saves the bid, and updates the high bid and end time together, so two simultaneous bids cannot both win and a bid is never accepted but unsaved. Why: Postgres is then the only source of truth, with no gap to repair when the API machine stops (the Fly trial does every 5 minutes); one hot auction is limited to a few hundred bids a second by the row lock, which is far above what this project needs. Redis is still used for rate limits and WebSocket fan-out. Bid placement sits behind one service, so it can move to Redis later if load ever needs it.
- Auth.js (free) issues a JWT that NestJS verifies with the shared secret, for HTTP and WebSocket connections. Admin and storefront share the user table; admins have `role = ADMIN`.
- Postgres on Neon. Redis and QStash on Upstash (NestJS needs a standard TCP Redis connection for the websocket adapter). Why: the owner doesn't care which, and this fits Vercel and QStash.
- Typesense indexes only live and upcoming auctions, synced from Postgres via the outbox. Ended auctions drop out. Price refresh is throttled to every few seconds per auction.
- Zustand holds only live client state (current high bid, bid feed over the WebSocket). Server data goes through server components and a query library, with no duplication in Zustand.

## Visual style

- Decided with the owner on 2026-10-30, after comparing mockups inspired by eBay, Yahoo Auctions Japan, and ZenMarket.
- Dense, information-first layout in the style of Yahoo Auctions Japan: facts in tables (price, bids, watchers, time left, auto-extension, minimum next bid), compact spacing, and a prominent bid input with a place-bid button.
- Brand accent: teal `#0f766e`, used for the header, links, buttons, and tags.
- Urgency (ending soon, final minutes) uses its own red/orange colour, separate from the brand accent, so it stays distinct whatever the brand colour is.
- The countdown is one shared timer synced to the server, so the listing page and bid history never disagree. On the listing page it follows the server's clock (the API sends its time with the listing), so everyone sees the same time left; the cards on the home and search pages still count from the viewer's clock.
- Countdown colour (added 2026-11-02): green while more than 50% of the auction's total time is left, orange from 50% down to 10%, and the urgency red at 10% or less. On the main page's cards it shows alone; on the listing page it is a "Time left" row for live auctions.
- Each app still has its own theme tokens (section 5). The admin theme is not chosen yet.

## 6. Quality and process

- Unit tests; integration tests against real Postgres and Redis in Docker; a concurrency test (50 simultaneous bids, exactly one wins); a few Playwright end-to-end tests (sign up, list, bid, win, pay).
- GitHub Actions runs lint, typecheck, and tests through Turborepo's cache on every PR.
- Environments: local (Docker Compose for Postgres and Redis), preview (Vercel previews with a staging backend), production. Stripe in test mode everywhere.
- Seed script: users, listings with images, auctions in every state (upcoming, live, ending in 1 minute, ended), simulated bidders. Plus a bot-bidder dev script that bids on a chosen auction so realtime can be watched solo. Built so far (2026-11-02): `yarn workspace @auction/db seed [count]` (default 1000, up to 20000, 0 removes it) creates 40 sample sellers (emails ending `@seed.local`, passwords that cannot sign in) and listings across all categories, conditions, prices and states; re-running replaces only its own data, and it refuses to run unless the database is local. Run `yarn workspace @auction/api reindex` afterwards. Bids (built 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 8): the same command also gives live and ended sample listings bids from the other sample users, following the real rules (the first at the starting price, each next at the minimum, nobody twice in a row, never the seller; about 30% of them get none and a few become a 25-bid war), with the listing's current bid, bid count and high bidder kept in agreement; upcoming listings get none. Removing sample data deletes the sample bids first, works out again any real listing a sample user had bid on from the bids that remain, then deletes the sample listings and accounts. The bot-bidder script `yarn workspace @auction/api bot <listingId> [--every <seconds>] [--max <bids>]` (default every 4 seconds, with some variation) bids on one live auction as the sample users through the HTTP API, so live updates, toasts, outbid emails and search sync can be watched by hand; it refuses to run unless the API is local.

## 7. Open decisions (decide in the relevant slice, then record here)

- Data model: tables, fields, and indexes. Designed per slice. Slice 1 added `User` (id, unique email, first name, last name, unique lowercase username, password hash, `emailVerified`, `role` of `USER` or `ADMIN`, timestamps) and `LoginCode` (user, HMAC of the 6-digit code, expiry, failed-attempt count). Auth.js uses JWT sessions with email and password, so it has no `Account` or `Session` tables. Slice 2 added `Category` (10 rows inserted by the migration: Collectibles, Watches & Jewelry, Electronics, Fashion, Home & Garden, Art, Books & Media, Toys & Games, Sports & Outdoors, Other), `Listing` (seller, title, description, category, condition of `NEW`/`LIKE_NEW`/`GOOD`/`FAIR`/`POOR`, starting price and optional reserve in cents, duration of 1/3/5/7/30 days, `status` of `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, `startsAt`/`endsAt`/`publishedAt`) and `ListingImage` (listing, URL, position; position 0 is the cover). Upcoming, live, and ended are derived from `startsAt` and `endsAt` (`listingPhase` in `packages/shared`); slice 5 adds the closed state. Extend per slice.
- Role freshness: the role is copied into a 30-day session JWT at sign-in, and the API trusts it. Before slice 6 (admin) or any ban feature, decide how the API gets a current role (for example, reading it from the database for admin routes) so a demoted admin or banned user doesn't keep access.
- Sign-in: **an emailed 6-digit code on every sign-in** (decided 2026-10-30, replacing the sign-up verification link). Sign-up sends a code straight away and goes to the code page, so a new user does not re-enter credentials. Later sign-ins are two steps: password, then the code. A correct code verifies the email, so every signed-in user has a verified email. Codes expire after 10 minutes, are single-use, lock after 5 wrong attempts, and a new code voids the old one. Signing up again with an unverified email replaces that account's name and password and voids its code, so nobody can block an address they don't own; a verified email returns 409. A cooldown stops email flooding (built 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 6): after a code is sent, asking for another for the same account (sign-in or signing up again) answers 429 `CODE_COOLDOWN` with the seconds left until 30 seconds have passed (`LOGIN_CODE_COOLDOWN_SECONDS`). It is only checked after the password is right, so it reveals nothing about who has an account.
- Username squatting: an unverified sign-up holds its username until it is replaced or deleted, so someone could hold handles without verifying. Decide on a cleanup rule for stale unverified accounts (for example, delete after a few days) before launch.
- Email sending: the API sends through Resend directly for now (`RESEND_API_KEY`, `MAIL_FROM`); moving sends behind QStash, as planned in section 4, comes with the notification work in slices 4 and 5. Without a verified domain Resend's sandbox sender `onboarding@resend.dev` delivers only to the Resend account's own email address, so other users can't receive codes until a domain is verified. In production the API refuses to start without a key rather than print codes to the log. A failed send returns 503.
- Query library for server data: React Query or SWR.
- Test runner: **Vitest** (decided 2026-10-30, slice 1).
- Package manager: **Yarn 4** with the `node-modules` linker (decided 2026-10-30, slice 1). Docker Desktop provides local Postgres and Redis.
- API host: **Fly.io on the free trial, no payment card** (decided 2026-10-30). App `auction-api-leke`, region `fra` (Frankfurt, next to the Neon database), deployed from `apps/api/Dockerfile` with `fly deploy -c apps/api/fly.toml --ha=false`. The trial stops the machine 5 minutes after each start, and Fly's proxy starts it again on the next request, which adds about 3 to 4 seconds for that request. This is accepted for testing and the owner will not add a card. Consequence for slice 4: the API can disappear mid-request at any time, so bidding must tolerate it the same way it tolerates a dropped connection (idempotency key on every bid, re-check state after a failure, auto-reconnect with resync of the live feed, no state held only in API memory). Other hosting: web on Vercel (`auction-web`), database on Neon (Frankfurt), email through Resend.
- Anti-sniping parameters: **2-minute window, 2-minute extension, at most 24 hours of extra time past the original end** (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4; the owner first chose a flat 15 minutes with no cap, then two steps, then this, after a review of how other sites do it). They are `ANTI_SNIPING` in `packages/shared`. The ceiling is not stored: it is the listing's `startsAt` plus its duration plus 24 hours (`extensionCeiling`). `Listing.extensionCount` is only a count of how many times a late bid moved the end (the database checks that it is not negative). The auction is in its "final chance" state once its end time has reached the ceiling (`isFinalChance`); the API sends that as `finalChance`, and the search index keeps it as `final_chance`. The index has to be updated on every extension (step 7), or a card keeps a stale tag or end time.
- Bid rule details (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4): the bid transaction judges live, ended and extension by the database's clock (`clock_timestamp()`, read after the row lock is won), not the API machine's, so several machines agree; the minimum next bid is `current + max(€1, 5% of current)` with the 5% rounded up to a whole cent, and the first bid only needs to meet the starting price. A bid must be at or above the minimum; a seller cannot bid on their own listing; only live auctions take bids; you cannot outbid yourself while you hold the high bid; bids are binding. Every bid carries an idempotency key, unique per bidder, so a retry after a dropped connection or a stopped API cannot place it twice. Bid API (slice 4, step 2): `POST /listings/:id/bids` with `{ amountCents, idempotencyKey }` (sign-in required) answers 201 for a new bid and 200 with `replayed: true` for a retry of one that already went through; a refusal carries a `code` and, for a bid below the minimum, `minimumNextBidCents`: 404 `NOT_FOUND`, 403 `OWN_LISTING`, 409 `NOT_LIVE` / `ALREADY_HIGHEST` / `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, 422 `TOO_LOW`. `GET /listings/:id/bids` is the public history, highest first, with bidder usernames. A listing that has a bid cannot be removed (409). The search index shows the current bid and bid count when a listing is next synced; updating it on every bid (throttled) is step 7. Hardening (2026-11-02, found by testing and review): a bid may be at most EUR 10,000,000 (`MAX_BID_CENTS`), so the next minimum always fits a 32-bit column; above about EUR 9.5 million nobody can outbid, which is accepted. A database failure during a bid is answered 404 (the listing or bidder vanished) or 503 `BUSY` ("your bid was not placed; try again", always safe to retry with the same key) and not a bare 500. A draft's details are written only while it is still a draft, so editing a listing that was just published answers 409 and cannot change terms under bidders. **A listing or an account that has bids cannot be deleted**: the database refuses (`Restrict` on `Bid` and on `Listing.highBidder`), and a listing with a bid must name its high bidder, so no deletion can erase a bid or leave a current bid nobody holds. When account deletion is built it must refuse or anonymise accounts that have bids.
- Realtime (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 4): a Socket.IO gateway in the API, WebSocket transport only (no long-polling, so several API machines need no sticky sessions), with the Redis adapter when `REDIS_URL` is set. Rooms: `auction:<listingId>`, which anyone may watch with a `watch` message, and `user:<userId>`, which the server alone joins a socket to from a verified token (a bad token is disconnected; the room name never comes from the client). The browser cannot read the httpOnly session, so a signed-in page asks `apps/web/app/api/realtime-token` for a 5-minute HS256 token and sends it when connecting (a fresh one on every reconnect). A connection may watch at most 20 auctions. Events carry the bidder's name taken inside the bid transaction and are sent without waiting on anything else, so they leave in the order the bids were saved. Events, published only after the bid transaction commits and never for a replay: `bid:placed` (price, bid count, high bidder, minimum next bid, end time, extension count, server time, the history row) and `auction:extended`. A failed push is logged and never fails the bid. The page keeps only this live state in a Zustand store and ignores any message older than what it holds; after every connect (the first included, since bids can land while the page loads), or if it sees a gap in the bid count, it refetches the page (`router.refresh()`), since the Fly machine stops every 5 minutes. The browser reaches the API at `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_URL` (default `http://localhost:4000`).
- Bid notifications (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 5): after a bid commits, the API sends the bidder who was outbid a `notice:outbid` message to their private room at once, plus an email that waits (see the next entry), and, when the bid moved the end time, a `notice:extended` message to every other bidder on that auction (not the new high bidder, the outbid bidder or the seller). Nobody else gets an email. Nothing is sent for a first bid, a refused bid or a replay, and a failed email or push is logged and never fails the bid. The email goes straight through the mail sender for now (QStash comes with slice 5). In the browser, every page for a signed-in user shows the toasts (bottom right, light rounded cards with a teal bar that shrinks over 5 seconds, and a clock icon on extension toasts; no red): the outbid toast has a "Bid €minimum" button that asks "Bid €X? A bid cannot be withdrawn." with Confirm and Cancel before bidding (two clicks, the clock waits while it asks; with a kept idempotency key; if the minimum has moved it shows the reason and the new minimum), and every toast disappears after 5 seconds (the clock stops while a bid is being confirmed or is in flight), and the extension toast is skipped on that listing's own page, which already shows the banner. Notices missed while disconnected are not replayed; the email covers outbid. A place to read past notifications and messages in the app is planned and will be built with slice 4b (decided with the owner, 2026-11-02). It keeps the same events as the toasts (outbid, auction extended) plus messages and questions, with an unread count. The API will save each notification when it creates it, and the toast becomes the live display of a saved one. Notifications expire after a few days; when an auction is completed its notifications expire too, except the latest "You've been outbid" one, which can stay. A notification can be marked as read, and clicking one opens its listing. The exact number of days and the layout are for slice 4b.
- Bid limits and audit log (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 6): every bid attempt that passes validation counts against 10 per minute per user and 30 per minute per address (`BID_LIMIT_USER`, `BID_LIMIT_IP`), refused attempts included, in a fixed one-minute window kept in Redis (an in-memory count when there is no `REDIS_URL`). Over the limit answers 429 `RATE_LIMITED`. If Redis is unreachable the attempt is allowed and a warning is logged, because the database still decides every bid. Every attempt writes one `BidAudit` row: accepted bids (and replays, with reason `REPLAY`) inside the bid's own transaction, refusals right after with the refusal code as the reason (`ERROR` for anything unexpected); a failure to write a refusal never changes the answer. Malformed requests (400) and signed-out requests (401) are not recorded. A retry of a bid that already went through is answered (200) before the limits are checked and does not count against them (changed 2026-11-03), so a bidder on a poor connection can always find out and is not blocked from a new bid by retries; and a flood being limited writes one `RATE_LIMITED` audit row per user per minute, not one per request. The address: bids reach the API from the web server, so the web server passes the visitor's address (Vercel's `x-real-ip`) in `x-viewer-ip` with an HMAC signature made with `AUTH_SECRET` in `x-viewer-ip-signature`; the API believes the address only with a valid signature and otherwise uses the connection's own, and `TRUST_PROXY` (1 on Fly) tells Express how many proxies to believe. Keep the audit rows for as long as bids are kept; no clean-up is built.
- The current high bidder cannot raise their own bid (decided 2026-11-02): it is refused with `ALREADY_HIGHEST`, and the listing page shows "You are the highest bidder" instead of the bid box.
- Outbid email timing (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, after step 6): an outbid email is queued in the `PendingOutbidEmail` table and sent by a sweeper (every 30 seconds, and at start-up) instead of at once, so a bidding war collapses into one email or none. It is due 3 minutes after the outbid (`OUTBID_EMAIL_DELAY_SECONDS`), or at once if the auction ends within that time, so a late outbid is never swallowed by the auction ending. When due it is skipped if the person is the high bidder again, or has that auction's page open (a socket of theirs in the `auction:<id>` room); bidding again deletes the waiting row. After one is sent, no other for the same person and auction for 15 minutes (`OUTBID_EMAIL_QUIET_SECONDS`, kept with the Redis rate limiter). It shows the numbers as they are when sent, and if the auction has already ended it says so with the final price. A send leases its row (moves `dueAt` 2 minutes out) and deletes it only after the email has gone, so a failed send or a machine that stops mid-send is tried again, up to 3 times (the 15-minute slot is given back when a send fails); waiting emails survive a stopped API machine (they go out when it next runs); the sweeper becomes a QStash delayed message in slice 5. Toasts are not delayed or limited. Known gap, closed in slice 5 step 2: in a long war you got one early email and then nothing as it ended; the close now sends every bidder who did not win an "auction ended" email (see "Ended emails and notification expiry").
- Reserve price: **the amount is visible to everyone** on the listing (decided 2026-10-30, slice 2). If it is not met when the auction closes there is no sale and the seller can relist (applied in slice 5).

Drafts are private to the seller and fully editable. After publishing, only the description and photos can change (at least one photo must stay). A draft can be deleted, and a seller can remove a published listing (deleting it, its photos, and its search entry) until it has a bid (checked and deleted in one statement, so a bid that lands at the same moment is never deleted with the listing). (Decided 2026-10-30, slice 2; removal of published listings added 2026-11-02.)

- Search host: **self-hosted Typesense in Docker Compose, not Typesense Cloud** (decided 2026-11-02, slice 3). Cloud's free allowance is 720 hours (about 30 days of one cluster), one time per account, and it then bills; whether signup needs a card is unconfirmed. The owner won't add a card. The Fly trial machine stops every 5 minutes, so it cannot host Typesense either. The code talks to Typesense through a `SearchIndex` interface and a URL and key from config, so a Cloud cluster can be swapped in later without code changes. Without `TYPESENSE_URL` and `TYPESENSE_API_KEY` (decided 2026-11-03), the API searches Postgres directly (`PostgresSearchIndex`) with the same filters and sorts, so deployed environments have working search for free; it is built to give the same results as Typesense (changed 2026-11-03, owner's request): whole words in the title or description with the start of the last word allowed, typo tolerance (1 typo from 4 letters, 2 from 6, through Postgres's `fuzzystrmatch` extension, enabled by the `fuzzy_search` migration; Neon supports it), the synonyms in `synonyms.ts` (two-way groups and one-way roots, including multi-word phrases), repeated letters cut to two, and, when no listing matches every word, the listings matching the most words (Typesense's token dropping). Title matches rank first. It no longer matches inside a word ("ring" finds ring but not string), as Typesense does not. Known differences: a swapped pair of letters counts as two typos in Postgres and one in Typesense, and the fuzzy match reads every listing's words, which is fine for thousands of live listings but would need a trigram index for many more. `apps/api/test/search-parity.e2e.test.ts` runs the same listings and the same typed text through both and requires equal results. Setting the two variables switches to Typesense with no code change.
- Search index sync: **direct from the API, not through the outbox** (decided 2026-11-02, slice 3). The outbox and QStash delivery do not exist yet, so after a listing is saved the API updates Typesense itself (`syncSearch` in `ListingsService`). Bids (decided 2026-11-02, slice 4, step 7): a `BidPlaced` event updates the indexed document's price, bid count, final-chance flag and end time with a partial update (`SearchSyncService`, listening to the in-process events). - Contacting the seller (decided 2026-11-03, slice 4b; built the same day). Public Q&A on the listing only: private threads and an inbox were dropped by the owner, since questions cover it. **Rules:** any signed-in user may ask, except the seller on their own listing; no bid needed. Questions are up to 1,000 characters and answers up to 2,000; sellers cannot turn questions off. A question can be asked only on a published auction that has not ended, and the seller answers once. The writer can edit or delete their own question or answer until the auction ends (a delete asks first); a seller cannot delete a buyer's question, but can take back their own answer, which reopens the question. After the end nothing can be asked, edited or deleted, though a seller can still post a first answer. An edit shows "(edited)", sends no notification, keeps the votes, and saving the same text changes nothing. A deleted question is gone from the list and the count (no placeholder). A short warning sits above the question box ("Keep conversations on the site; never share payment details"). **Data and API:** a `Question` table (listing, asker, body, `deletedAt`, `editedAt`, and the seller's single `answer` with `answeredAt` / `answerEditedAt`). `GET /listings/:id/questions` (public; with a valid token it also returns the reader's own vote), `POST /listings/:id/questions`, `PATCH /questions/:id`, `POST` / `PATCH` / `DELETE /questions/:id/answer`, `DELETE /questions/:id`. When user deletion is built, `Question.askerId` becomes null (`onDelete: SetNull`) and the question shows "<deleted user>" with "This message can't be displayed because the user was deleted." while the answer, votes and count stay; the API already hides that text, but delete-account must also blank the stored text. **Notifications:** a saved question notifies the seller and a saved answer the asker, with a live toast (`notice:question` / `notice:answer`), a bell notification (`QUESTION_ASKED` / `QUESTION_ANSWERED`, keyed `question:<id>` / `answer:<id>`) and an email with the full text, driven by `QuestionAsked` / `QuestionAnswered` events on `QuestionEvents` (`QuestionNotificationsService`). A failure to save, push or email is logged and never fails the post. **Votes:** every question and seller answer has a like and a dislike with a count each (`QuestionVote`, one per person and target). `PUT /questions/:id/vote` with `{ target, value }` sets, changes or takes back (`value: null`) a vote and answers the new counts. The asker cannot vote on their own question (403 `OWN_POST`), the seller cannot vote at all (403 `SELLER_VOTE`), signed-out visitors see counts only. A deleted question hides its votes; taking back an answer deletes the votes on it. **Limits and reports:** 10 questions a minute and 10 reports a minute per person (429 `RATE_LIMITED`, in Redis; refused questions do not count). "Report" in the three-dot menu on someone else's post asks for a reason (spam or advertising, rude or abusive, shares contact details, something else) and saves a `Report` (`POST /questions/:id/report` with `{ target, reason }`) holding the post, reporter, reason, the words as they were, `postedAt` and status `OPEN` or `HANDLED`. Nothing is hidden and nobody is told. One report per person and post (409 `ALREADY_REPORTED`), not on your own post (403 `SELF_REPORT`); the seller may report a question and the asker an answer, and `postedAt` lets a person report a replacement answer. Reports have no foreign keys; open ones are never cleaned up and outlive the post, listing and accounts until the admin app (slice 6) handles them, and handled ones go 30 days later. **Contact details:** a new or edited post that seems to hold an email address or phone number (8 to 15 digits; dates and prices excluded; `containsContactDetails` in `packages/shared`) is published as normal and also saved as a `CONTACT_DETAILS` report with no reporter, one open report per post whose words follow later edits. **Admin log:** every post as first written, every edit (with the previous text) and every deletion (with the deleted text) is written to `QuestionLog` in the same transaction (question, listing, which post, action, who, text, previous text, time). It has no foreign keys and the admin app (slice 6) will read it behind the `ADMIN` check. `QuestionLog` entries, and questions their askers deleted, are removed 30 days after the auction ends (`QUESTION_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS`) by `QuestionRetentionService` at start-up and hourly; entries whose listing is gone count from when they were written.

Built 2026-11-03 (slice 4b, step 1): a `Notification` table (user, listing, `type` of `OUTBID` or `AUCTION_EXTENDED`, a `key` that is unique per user so a newer notice about the same thing replaces the older one and marks it unread, title, the live notice as `data`, `readAt`, `expiresAt`). - Slice 5 build order (decided 2026-11-04): close logic first, then the "you did not win" emails, orders with the fake `PaymentProvider`, Stripe test mode, payment expiry with strikes, one-click relist, and QStash last, behind a `JobScheduler` interface. - Closing an auction (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 1): `AuctionCloseService` (`apps/api/src/auctions`) closes a published auction once its end time has passed. It is one `UPDATE` that only matches an unclosed auction whose `endsAt` has passed by the database's clock, so a duplicate call, a second machine or the sweep catching up changes nothing, and a bid that is extending the auction at that moment (holding the row lock) keeps it open. It sets `Listing.closedAt`, `outcome` (`SOLD`, `NO_SALE` when the top bid is below the reserve, or `NO_BIDS`), and for a sale `winnerId` and `finalPriceCents` (the winner has the final bid; winner and high bidder are the same person). It then publishes `AuctionClosed` on the in-process events. A sweep at start-up and every 10 seconds is the safety-net trigger (the only one when QStash is not configured; see "QStash jobs"), so the result appears within about 10 seconds of the end; on the Fly trial, where the machine stops every 5 minutes, an auction that ends while it is stopped is closed when it next starts (the sweep also runs at start-up), which is one more reason QStash is added; the listing page shows "Closing the auction..." and asks for the page again every 10 seconds until it is saved, then shows the result in a "Result" row. The migration closed existing ended auctions the same way, without events. `GET /listings/:id` carries `closedAt`, `outcome`, `winner` and `finalPriceCents`. Ended auctions already drop out of search by end time, so closing does not touch the index.
- Ended emails and notification expiry (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 2): closing an auction also, in the same transaction, (1) owes an "auction ended" email to every distinct bidder except the winner (a `PendingEndEmail` row per person and auction), (2) deletes any outbid email still waiting for that auction, so nobody gets two, and (3) expires the auction's saved notifications at once (hidden immediately, deleted by the hourly clean-up): every type, except each loser's latest "You've been outbid", which stays out its normal 14 days; the winner's outbid notice expires too. `AuctionEndEmailService` sends what is owed (a sweep every 30 seconds, and straight after a close), with the same lease and 3 tries as the outbid emails, so a failed send or a stopped machine delays the email and never loses it. A sale says "you did not win" with the final price; when the reserve was not met, every bidder, the top one included, is told the auction ended without a sale and the highest bid; an auction with no bids sends nothing. The unread count on an open page is not pushed when notifications expire; it is right on the next load.
- Orders, the fee and payment (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 3): closing a sale also creates, in the same transaction, an `Order` for the winner (`listingId` + `round`, `buyerId`, `sellerId`, `amountCents`, `feeCents`, `sellerNetCents`, `status` of `AWAITING_PAYMENT`/`PAID`/`EXPIRED`/`CANCELLED`, `payBy`, `paidAt`, `paymentRef`, `paymentRefs`) and owes the winner a "you won: pay now" email (`PendingEndEmail` `kind` `WON`; `LOST` for the others). The fee is `PLATFORM_FEE_PERCENT` (default 5) of the final price, a half cent rounded up (`platformFeeCents` in `packages/shared`), taken from the seller. The buyer has `PAYMENT_WINDOW_HOURS` (default 48) to pay, counted from the database's `closedAt`. No sale means no order, and sales closed before orders existed have none. **API** (sign-in required; only the order's buyer and seller see it, anyone else gets 404): `GET /orders` (optionally `?listingId=`), `GET /orders/:id` (the fee and seller's share go to the seller only), `POST /orders/:id/checkout` (buyer only, while waiting and before `payBy`; creates a checkout session, answers `{ url }`; refusals `NOT_BUYER` 403, `NOT_PAYABLE` and `PAYMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` 409), `POST /orders/:id/confirm` (buyer only; asks the provider and marks the order `PAID` once; safe to repeat) and `POST /orders/:id/test-pay` (fake provider only, and only when `NODE_ENV` is unset, `development` or `test`). Every checkout session is kept in `paymentRefs` and `confirm` checks them all, so paying on an earlier tab is found; a payment landing after `payBy` is accepted while the order is still `AWAITING_PAYMENT`. `PaymentProvider` (`apps/api/src/payments`) has `createCheckout`, `confirm`, `refund` and `payout` (used in slice 6); tests replace it through the `PAYMENT_PROVIDER` token. **Web:** `/orders/[id]` (the buyer sees the price and "Pay"; the seller sees the fee, their share and "Waiting for the buyer to pay"), `/orders/[id]/checkout` (the fake provider's "Pay with test card" page), and the listing page's bid panel points the winner ("You won it for €X. Pay now") and the seller ("This item sold. View the order") to the order. The order page checks a payment once, then reloads without `?paid=`, and says so when the check fails. **Won toast:** when a sale closes, the winner's open pages get `notice:won` and a "You won the auction" toast with a "Go to the auction" button; live only, not saved to the bell or replayed (the email covers a miss). The outbid email no longer sends once an auction has ended; the close's email covers it. The close sweep leaves a listing that failed to close out for 5 minutes so broken ones cannot fill the batch, and an auction-ended email whose result is not saved yet is retried, not dropped.
- Stripe payments (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 4): `StripePaymentProvider` (`apps/api/src/payments`) is chosen when both `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` are set, and the in-memory fake otherwise (`PaymentsModule`). Checkout is Stripe's hosted page for a one-time EUR payment (`mode: payment`, one line item for the amount, `client_reference_id` and `metadata.orderId` set to the order id, `success_url` `/orders/:id?paid=1`, which the order page confirms, and `cancel_url` back to the order). `confirm` retrieves the session and reports paid when `payment_status` is `paid`; `refund` refunds the session's payment intent with the idempotency key `refund_<sessionId>` and does nothing for an unpaid session. `payout` refuses with an error, because paying a seller needs a connected Stripe account and sellers have no onboarding; how sellers are paid is for slice 6. Webhook: `POST /payments/stripe/webhook` (no sign-in; the API is created with `rawBody` so the signature is checked on the exact bytes) handles `checkout.session.completed` and `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`. It marks an order `PAID` only if the session is paid, the order is still `AWAITING_PAYMENT`, the session is one of that order's attempts, and the amount and currency (EUR) match; a repeat, a late payment on an expired order, or anything else changes nothing and answers 200. Repeats are made safe by that guarded update rather than a table of event ids. A payment that arrives after the order expired is refunded (see "Paying exactly once" under step 4b). Locally, `stripe listen --forward-to localhost:4000/payments/stripe/webhook` gives the `whsec_` value.
- Delivery, confirmation email and invoice (decided and built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 4b; moved forward from slice 6 at the owner's request, so tracking, "seller marks shipped" and payout stay in slice 6). **Flow:** "Pay now" opens `/orders/:id/shipping`: the buyer enters name, street, city, postcode, country and phone, picks a delivery option, and goes straight to checkout for item plus delivery. **Price:** the platform handles delivery itself and pays the carriers from what the buyer pays; there is no carrier integration and may never be. Delivery = 5% of the item price, never below EUR 3 or above EUR 50 (`DELIVERY_PERCENT`, `DELIVERY_MIN_CENTS`, `DELIVERY_MAX_CENTS`), plus the option's extra: Standard +EUR 0 (about 6 days, not tracked), Express +EUR 6 (about 2 days, tracked and insured), Tracked and insured +EUR 4 (about 6 days, tracked and insured). `DELIVERY_OPTIONS`, `deliveryBaseCents` and `deliveryOptions` in `packages/shared/src/shipping.ts` let web and API price the same way. Delivery money is never part of the seller fee (5% of the item, capped at `PLATFORM_FEE_CAP_CENTS`, default 5000, for orders created from now on) or of the seller's share. **Pickup address:** a seller saves a default pickup address and contact (name, street, city, postcode, country, phone) in account settings (`GET/PUT /me/pickup-address`, `User.pickupAddress`). Each listing carries its own `pickupAddress`, starting from the default and changeable per item ("Save as my default" on the listing form). It is required to publish, copied onto the order at close, and sent only to the seller. The seller can correct it at any time, even after a sale (`PUT /listings/:id/pickup-address`, on the edit page and the seller's order page), and orders for the listing that are waiting or paid take the correction. The seller's "sold" email names the address with a correction link. **Who sees what:** the buyer sees their own delivery address and never the pickup address. The seller sees the pickup address, the delivery option and only the buyer's city and country, never their street or phone, and never what the buyer paid in all or for delivery: their order page, email and PDF show the item price, the fee and their share (the API sends the seller `totalCents: null`), and their PDF is a "sale statement". The admin app (slice 6) reads the full order. **API:** `GET /orders/:id/shipping/options` lists the options with this order's prices; `PUT /orders/:id/shipping` (address, `optionId`) recomputes the price server-side and saves it; both are buyer-only, while waiting and before `payBy`. Checkout refuses with `SHIPPING_REQUIRED` until then and charges `totalCents` as two Stripe line items; an order is paid only when the amount equals `totalCents`. `Order` gains `shippingAddress`, `shippingOption`, `shippingCents`, `totalCents`, `pickupAddress` and `invoiceNumber`. **Address rules (`shippingAddressSchema`):** names and cities allow only letters, spaces, dots, hyphens and apostrophes, so neither side can slip the other a message or markup; street lines refuse line breaks and `@`; the postcode must fit the country; the phone is international (a country-code selector that follows the country), stored as `+<code><number>` without spaces, 7 to 15 digits, leading 0 dropped after the code (Italy keeps it). **Paying exactly once:** an order has one open checkout page at a time. Paying again (second tab, double click, backing out) sends the buyer to the page already open, and the order row is locked while a page is opened. Saving the same delivery leaves it alone; changing the total closes it (`PaymentProvider.expireCheckout`) so the old total can never be paid. When an order is paid, its other sessions are closed or, if paid too, refunded. Any payment that does not match the order (second session, old total, other currency, expired order) is refunded (`OrdersService.settlePayment`); a failed refund makes the webhook answer an error so Stripe retries. Checkout is card only. **After payment:** the transaction that marks the order `PAID` also takes the invoice number (`INV-<year>-<six digits>`, from `InvoiceNumberSeq`) and queues two emails (`PendingEndEmail` kinds `PAID` and `SOLD`) for the existing leased sweep. The buyer's email confirms payment and lists the lines, delivery and address, with the PDF attached (`MailSender.send` takes attachments). The seller's says the carrier will collect from the pickup address, so pack the item and keep the phone on, and shows their share. The PDF (`pdfkit`) is served by `GET /orders/:id/invoice` to the buyer and seller of a paid order, proxied at `/orders/:id/invoice`; it is a receipt, not a tax invoice. Orders created before this cannot be paid until the buyer chooses delivery; earlier listings have no pickup address.
- Payment expiry, the runner-up offer and strikes (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 5). `OrderExpiryService` (`apps/api/src/orders`) gives up on orders still `AWAITING_PAYMENT` after `payBy`: a sweep at start-up and every 30 seconds, plus `expire(orderId)`. Each expiry is one transaction that only matches an order that is still waiting and past its deadline, so duplicate runs and several machines change nothing twice: the order becomes `EXPIRED`; the buyer gets a `Strike` (`orderId` unique, so one missed payment counts once); the item is offered to the next bidder at **their own highest bid** (a new `Order` with `round + 1`, a fresh window from the expiry, the fee on that amount, the listing's pickup address, and the listing's winner and final price follow it); or, if nobody qualifies, the listing's `outcome` becomes `NO_SALE`. A bidder qualifies if never offered the item, not blocked, and their highest bid meets the reserve (if the best remaining bid is below it, all are). The chain repeats until someone pays or no bid is left. Before expiring, the service asks the provider about the order's sessions (`OrdersService.settleIfPaid`): a payment taken but not yet reported pays the order and earns no strike, and if the provider cannot be asked the order waits and the sweep leaves it alone for 5 minutes before trying again; if it still cannot be asked 24 hours after the deadline, the order is expired anyway (a payment that lands later is refunded), so one unreadable session cannot hold up the strike and the runner-up offer for ever (changed 2026-11-04, after review). The strike count in the non-payer's email is the count as of that order, not today's. The strike count check on a bid is read before the listing row is locked, so it does not lengthen the lock. The deadline and the new buyer's `payBy` use the database's clock, and a pay-now, expiry or not-paid email of the same kind still waiting from an earlier round is made due again instead of being dropped. `GET /orders` lists the newest round first. Open checkout pages are closed before the order is expired (changed 2026-11-04, after review): if one cannot be closed, nothing is expired and the sweep tries again, so the buyer can never pay for an item already offered on, until the 24-hour give-up when a payment that still arrives is refunded. **Strikes:** never expire; an admin removes them in slice 6. At `MAX_STRIKES` (2, `packages/shared`) `POST /listings/:id/bids` refuses with `BLOCKED` (403, audit-logged), and a blocked bidder is skipped as runner-up. `GET /listings/:id` carries `viewerBlocked` and the listing page shows a note instead of the bid box. Existing bids and wins stand. **Emails** (leased `PendingEndEmail` rows, kinds `EXPIRED`, `NOT_PAID`, and `WON` for the runner-up): the non-payer is told their strike count ("strike 1 of 2", or that they can no longer bid) and that a later charge is refunded; the seller is told who did not pay and either who was offered the item next, at what price and until when, or that the auction ended without a sale and can be listed again; the runner-up gets the pay-now email worded as an offer and a live toast ("The item is yours to pay for", `WonNotice.offered`) from the `OrderExpired` event, live only like the won toast. The listing page tells the original buyer "You did not pay for it in time, so the order was cancelled" and the seller that the buyer did not pay when the chain ended in no sale.

- One-click relist (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 6): `POST /listings/:id/relist` (seller only) copies a closed listing whose outcome is `NO_SALE` or `NO_BIDS` into a new **draft** with the same title, description, category, condition, starting price, reserve, duration, pickup address and photos; anything else answers 409 (a sale, a running auction, a draft), and someone else's listing answers 403. It does not publish, so the seller can change anything first; the old listing stays as the record. The seller sees a "List it again" button in the owner banner on the closed listing page, which opens the new draft's edit page. Asking again while that draft exists returns it instead of making another (`Listing.relistedFromId`, with a lock so two simultaneous calls take turns); once the draft is published, a new ask makes a new draft. The copy shares photo files with the old listing, so the API leaves a photo out of `removedImageUrls` while any other listing still uses it (the web app deletes only what is reported).

- QStash jobs (built 2026-11-04, slice 5, step 7): scheduling sits behind a `JobScheduler` (`apps/api/src/jobs`) with `scheduleClose(listingId, at)` and `scheduleOrderExpiry(orderId, at)`. `JobSchedulerModule` (global) picks `QStashJobScheduler` when `QSTASH_TOKEN`, `QSTASH_CURRENT_SIGNING_KEY`, `QSTASH_NEXT_SIGNING_KEY` and `API_PUBLIC_URL` (the address QStash reaches the API at) are all set, and otherwise an in-process one that does nothing, because the sweeps (close every 10 seconds, expiry every 30) are then the scheduler. `QSTASH_URL` points the client at the local dev server. A message is published for just after the moment (1 second late, rounded up to a whole second, so the database clock has certainly passed it) with a deduplication id of the job and the time joined with hyphens (QStash refuses a colon in it; only the real server showed this, and without the fix every schedule would have failed quietly behind the sweeps), so asking twice for the same moment publishes once. Scheduling never throws: a failure is logged and the sweep covers it. **When it is asked:** when a listing is published (the close at `endsAt`); when a bid extends the auction (`AuctionExtended`, the close at the new end); when closing creates an order and when expiry offers the item on (`scheduleOrderExpiry` at `payBy`). **Calls back:** `POST /jobs/close/:id` and `POST /jobs/expire-order/:id` have no sign-in; `QStashSignatureGuard` checks QStash's `Upstash-Signature` (a JWT over the raw body and the exact address it called, verified with the current key and, failing that, the next key, 5 seconds of clock tolerance) and answers 401 otherwise and 404 when QStash is not configured. They run the same idempotent operations as the sweeps (`AuctionCloseService.close`, `OrderExpiryService.expire`), so a duplicate, early or stale message changes nothing; they answer 200 with `{ closed }` or `{ expired }`, including for an id that does not exist (so QStash does not retry for ever), and 5xx when the work fails, so QStash retries. **Hops:** QStash's free plan holds a message for at most 7 days and an auction can last 30, so a message is never set more than 6 days out; a call that finds the auction (or order) not yet due schedules the next one for its real end, which also handles the old message that arrives after an extension. The sweep stays as the safety net. Messages are not backfilled for auctions already live when QStash is first switched on: the sweep closes those, and only a stopped machine would be late. Not built: cancelling a message that an extension made stale (it arrives, finds the auction open and passes the baton), and moving the outbid and ended emails onto QStash: they stay as leased database rows with their sweeps, which already survive a stopped machine and retry, so QStash would add nothing but a dependency. **Checked against the real QStash dev server (2026-11-04):** a close message delivered to the API passed the signature check and closed the auction about 0.3 seconds after its end time (a sweep cannot do that), closing queued the 48-hour expiry message, an expiry message delivered at the deadline expired the order and struck the buyer, and the same close worked against the production Docker image run with `NODE_ENV=production`. **Production setup (Fly):** `fly secrets set QSTASH_TOKEN=... QSTASH_CURRENT_SIGNING_KEY=... QSTASH_NEXT_SIGNING_KEY=... API_PUBLIC_URL=https://auction-api-leke.fly.dev` with the values from the QStash page of the Upstash console (leave `QSTASH_URL` unset: it defaults to Upstash's server). `API_PUBLIC_URL` must be exactly the address QStash calls, because the signature names it. `fly.toml` keeps one machine running with auto-start on, so a call also finds a stopped machine and starts it. Without the four variables the API runs exactly as before, on the sweeps. **Live (2026-11-04):** the secrets are set on Fly with `QSTASH_URL=https://qstash-eu-central-1.upstash.io` (the account is in the EU region), the Neon database is migrated to all 30 migrations, and the deployed API accepted real signed calls from QStash EU (close job for an unknown listing answered 200; unsigned calls answer 401). The free plan allows 1,000 messages a day: a message per publish, per extension and per order, plus retries, which is far above this project's use.

## 8. Build order and done-criteria

Work in thin vertical slices. Each slice works end to end, with tests passing, before the next starts.

1. **Monorepo, DB, auth.** Done when: Turborepo builds all apps and packages; Docker Compose brings up Postgres and Redis; Prisma migrations run; a user can sign up, verify email, and sign in on `apps/web`; NestJS verifies the Auth.js JWT on a protected route; CI runs lint, typecheck, and tests.
2. **Listings and images.** Done when: a seller can create, edit, and publish a listing with images uploaded to Vercel Blob; categories are seeded; listing pages render.
3. **Search.** Done when: live and upcoming listings sync to Typesense; search has typo tolerance, filters, and sorting; ended auctions drop out of the index.
4. **Bidding and realtime.** Done when: users can place bids that obey the minimum rule; the page updates live over WebSocket; anti-sniping works; the outbid toast and email fire; the 50-concurrent-bids test passes with exactly one winner; rate limits and audit logging are in place.
4b. **Contacting the seller.** Done when: a signed-in user can ask a public question on a listing and the seller can answer it; each new question or answer gives the other side a live notification and an email; the seller cannot ask on their own listing; questions are rate-limited and can be reported. Private threads and an inbox were dropped on 2026-11-03. Built after slice 4 is done.
5. **Auction close and payment.** Done when: QStash closes auctions idempotently; the winner receives the pay-now email, enters a delivery address and picks a delivery option, pays the price plus delivery in Stripe test mode, and gets a confirmation email with a PDF invoice and the delivery details; the 48-hour expiry triggers the second-bidder offer and strikes; the platform fee is applied; every bidder who was outbid gets one "auction ended, you did not win" email with the final price.
6. **Shipping, notifications, admin.** Done when: the fake shipping flow completes an order and releases payout; the watchlist, "ending soon" emails, and seller ratings work; `apps/admin` supports moderation and the reports queue behind the `ADMIN` role.
