# Auction App: Design & Decisions

Status: design agreed with the owner in a grilling session on 2026-10-30. This file is the source of truth for decisions and their reasons. If you need to deviate from it, ask the owner first and update this file. Endpoint lists, field lists and step-by-step build logs live in the code and tests, not here.

## 1. Product

- Portfolio/learning project built to production-quality standards. It is not expected to launch, so there is no legal, tax, or compliance work.
- Multi-seller marketplace: one account type, every user can buy and sell.
- Reference countries: Kosovo, Albania, North Macedonia. Stripe availability there is unverified (Kosovo unclear). Payment and shipping providers therefore sit behind interfaces so they can be swapped.
- Currency: EUR only. Language: English only. Prices are integer cents. Timestamps are UTC, displayed in the user's timezone. `next-intl` and ALL/MKD can be added later.

## 2. Auction rules

- Timed English auctions with an optional reserve price. Durations: 1, 3, 5, 7, or 30 days.
- Bids are manual and binding. No proxy/max bidding in v1 (it complicates bid logic a lot).
- Minimum next bid = `current + max(€1, 5% of current)`, the 5% rounded up to a whole cent; the first bid only needs to meet the starting price. The user types any amount at or above it; the UI shows the minimum as a hint and the API enforces it. The 5% is config. The rule lives in `packages/shared` so web and API agree. Examples: €10 → €11; €100 → €105; €1,000 → €1,050. Why: the owner rejected fixed tiers and wanted free-entry bids, but not +1 cent nudges.
- Anti-sniping (decided 2026-11-02): a bid in the last 2 minutes sets the end to 2 minutes after that bid, every time, so a bidding war keeps going. The only limit is a ceiling: the end can never pass 24 hours after the original end (start plus duration). The ceiling is not stored (`extensionCeiling`); `Listing.extensionCount` only counts extensions; "final chance" is the state where the end has reached the ceiling. Parameters are `ANTI_SNIPING` in `packages/shared`.
- Sellers cannot bid on their own items (shill bidding is the main fraud risk). The current high bidder cannot raise their own bid (`ALREADY_HIGHEST`).
- Bids are rate-limited and audit-logged (see section 7). Bidding requires a verified email only, no saved payment method. Two strikes block bidding.

## 3. Close and payment

- QStash schedules an idempotent close job per auction (rescheduled on extension), with a periodic sweep as safety net. Idempotency stops a duplicate delivery closing or charging twice.
- The winner gets an email and a pay-now page with 48 hours to pay by card. Why: the owner wants payment after the auction, not a pre-authorization (card holds expire in about 7 days). Before paying, the buyer enters a delivery address and picks a delivery option; the charge is item price plus delivery. Our carrier collects from the seller's pickup address, so the seller posts nothing.
- If the window expires: the non-payer gets a strike and the item is offered to the next bidder (see section 7). The seller can relist with one click.
- Platform fee: 5% of the item price, capped at EUR 50 per sale, taken from the seller. No listing fees or buyer premium. Delivery money is not part of the fee.
- `PaymentProvider` interface (checkout, confirm, refund, payout): Stripe test mode as the main implementation, in-memory fake for tests.
- `ShippingProvider` interface: a fake provider moves orders `label created → in transit → delivered` on a timer. The seller marks the item shipped. Payout is released 3 days after delivery, by the timer only; there is no early buyer confirmation (decided 2026-11-05). Why: no real carrier integration, since the project won't go live.

## 4. Features

- Listings with up to 8 images (under 5 MB each), uploaded from the browser straight to Vercel Blob.
- Fixed list of 10 seeded categories.
- **Search:** Typesense with typo tolerance, filters (category, condition, price range, ending within 24 hours) and sorts (best match, ending soonest, ending latest, newest, most bids, fewest bids, price; "ending latest" and "fewest bids" added 2026-11-05). Only live and upcoming auctions appear, except that "ending latest" (like the "ending within 24 hours" filter) lists only live ones: an upcoming auction has not started (decided 2026-11-05). Synonyms are in `apps/api/src/search/synonyms.ts` (one-way general words such as "shoes" finding sneakers; spelling variants). Search is 503 when Typesense is down. The search page and home page share a sidebar of filters. The header search keeps filters as removable chips, is hidden on auth pages, and does nothing when empty. Recent searches (last 8) are kept in the browser's `localStorage` only. Suggestions as you type (2+ letters, 5 titles, debounced) use the same `SearchIndex.search`; any failure shows nothing. Arrow keys and Enter work on both lists. Suggestions have no rate limit of their own yet.
- **Bidding on the listing page:** current bid or starting price, bid count, high bidder, minimum next bid, reserve with "(met)"/"(not met)" once there are bids, and the latest 5 bids (`BID_HISTORY_SHOWN`). The bid box starts at the minimum and refuses lower amounts client-side. Every bid needs a second click ("A bid cannot be withdrawn"), with an extra warning at ten times the minimum or more. The bid carries an idempotency key kept while the outcome is unknown. Signed-out visitors, the seller, the high bidder, and upcoming/ended auctions see a note instead of the box. The page updates live over WebSocket.
- **Main page:** "Ending soon" (live, ending within 24 hours) and "Upcoming" sections of 4 cards above the full list on page 1; empty sections are hidden.
- **Accounts:** first name, last name, and a unique public username (3-20 chars of lowercase letters, numbers, underscores; some reserved; changeable). Real names stay private. Sign-in is by email. Settings (`/account/settings`): names, username, email, password, pickup address. Changing email or password needs the current password; an email change is confirmed by a 6-digit code to the new address. Every new-password field is entered twice. Forgot-password emails a one-hour, single-use link that also verifies the email and does not sign in.
- **Notifications:** outbid email plus a live toast with a one-click bid. When a late bid extends an auction, viewers see the countdown jump and an amber banner (kept for the rest of the auction while `extensionCount` > 0), the outbid bidder gets the usual notice with the new end time, and other bidders signed in elsewhere get a toast. The extension that reaches the ceiling is "Final chance to bid" (toast, and a yellow tag on cards; no banner, removed by the owner). There is deliberately no email to all bidders, to keep email volume low.
- **Emails** (Resend): outbid, won/pay-now, auction ended, payment confirmation, expiry, Q&A, and later ending-soon and shipping updates. Moving sends onto QStash was dropped (see section 7).
- **Contacting the seller:** public Q&A on the listing only. Private messages and an inbox are out of scope.
- Watchlist with "ending soon" emails; seller profiles with post-sale buyer ratings (slice 6).
- Report button on listings; admin app for moderation and the reports queue (slice 7).

Out of scope for v1: proxy bidding, buy-it-now, offers, private messages, push/SMS, nested categories, automated content moderation, real shipping carriers, multiple currencies or languages.

## 5. Architecture

Turborepo monorepo:

- `apps/web`: Next.js storefront (Vercel).
- `apps/admin`: separate Next.js app with its own theme and Vercel project. Uses admin-only API routes guarded by an `ADMIN` role in the API (never trust the UI). No public sign-up.
- `apps/api`: NestJS modular monolith with in-process domain events. Modules talk through interfaces and events, not each other's tables. Runs on a long-running container host because Vercel functions cannot hold WebSockets.
- `packages/db`: Prisma schema and client; the only package that imports Prisma.
- `packages/shared`: types, Zod schemas, bid and shipping rules.
- `packages/ui`: unstyled primitives (Tailwind + shadcn/ui); each app has its own theme tokens.
- `packages/config`: shared tsconfig and eslint.

Rules:

- Next.js apps get all data through the API and never touch Postgres.
- **Postgres decides each bid** (decided 2026-11-02, replacing a Redis Lua script plus reconciliation). One transaction locks the listing row, checks the minimum, saves the bid, and updates high bid and end time together. Why: Postgres is the only source of truth, with no gap to repair when the API machine stops. One hot auction tops out at a few hundred bids a second, far above need. Redis is used for rate limits and WebSocket fan-out. Bid placement sits behind one service so it can move later.
- Auth.js issues a JWT that NestJS verifies with the shared secret, for HTTP and WebSocket. Admin and storefront share the user table.
- Postgres on Neon; Redis and QStash on Upstash (NestJS needs standard TCP Redis).
- Typesense indexes only live and upcoming auctions; ended ones drop out.
- Zustand holds only live client state (high bid, bid feed). Server data goes through server components and a query library, with no duplication.

## Visual style

- Decided 2026-10-30 after comparing eBay, Yahoo Auctions Japan and ZenMarket mockups: dense, information-first layout like Yahoo Auctions Japan, facts in tables, compact spacing, a prominent bid input.
- Brand accent teal `#0f766e` (header, links, buttons, tags). Urgency uses its own red/orange so it stays distinct.
- Countdown: on the listing page it follows the server's clock (the API sends its time), so everyone agrees; cards count from the viewer's clock. Colour: green above 50% of total time left, orange from 50% to 10%, red at 10% or less.
- Each app has its own theme tokens. The admin theme is chosen (2026-11-05): light, slate and indigo. A white/grey table area, a dark slate sidebar (`#1f2433`), indigo accent (`#4f46e5`), amber/blue/green status pills; clearly different from the teal storefront.

## 6. Quality and process

- Unit tests (Vitest); integration tests against real Postgres and Redis in Docker; a concurrency test (50 simultaneous bids, exactly one wins); a few Playwright end-to-end tests (sign up, list, bid, win, pay).
- GitHub Actions runs lint, typecheck and tests through Turborepo's cache on every PR.
- Environments: local (Docker Compose), preview (Vercel previews with a staging backend), production. Stripe in test mode everywhere.
- Seed script `yarn workspace @auction/db seed [count]` (default 1000, 0 removes): sample sellers (`@seed.local`, cannot sign in) and listings in every state, with bids following the real rules. It replaces only its own data and refuses to run unless the database is local. Run `yarn workspace @auction/api reindex` afterwards. Bot bidder: `yarn workspace @auction/api bot <listingId> [--every <s>] [--max <n>]` bids through the HTTP API so realtime can be watched solo; local API only.

## 7. Decisions

Data model: designed per slice; see `packages/db/prisma/schema.prisma`. Upcoming/live/ended are derived from `startsAt`/`endsAt` (`listingPhase`); closing adds `closedAt` and `outcome`.

**Open**
- Role freshness (decided 2026-11-05, slice 7): the role is still copied into the 30-day JWT at sign-in, but the API no longer trusts it. `JwtAuthGuard` looks the user up in the database on every authenticated request (one primary-key query) and uses the database's role and email; a missing user is 401, a banned user (`User.bannedAt` set) is 403 `BANNED`, and `OptionalJwtAuthGuard` treats a banned user as signed out. A demoted admin or banned user therefore loses access on their next request, and a promoted user gains it at once. `AdminGuard` (after `JwtAuthGuard`) allows only `role = ADMIN`. A banned user cannot start a sign-in (403 `BANNED` after the right password) or finish one, and a socket connecting with a banned user's token is refused (the account is checked in a Socket.IO middleware, so the private room is joined before the client hears `connect`). A socket that is already connected when the ban lands keeps its private room until it reconnects; the ban action (step 4) disconnects it.
- Admin app shell and sign-in (built 2026-11-05, slice 7, step 2): `apps/admin` (port 3001) has its own Auth.js setup and the slate and indigo theme. Sign-in is the storefront's two steps (password, then the emailed 6-digit code) and only an account whose role is ADMIN gets a session; anyone else gets the same message as a wrong code. There is no sign-up: the first admin is made with `yarn workspace @auction/db make-admin <email>` (`--remove` demotes; it works on whatever `DATABASE_URL` points at). It uses its own cookie names (`admin.session-token`, `admin.csrf-token`, `admin.callback-url`) because on localhost the storefront and the admin app share cookies, and sets `AUTH_URL` to `ADMIN_URL` (default `http://localhost:3001`) so the storefront's value in the shared `.env` does not leak in. Every console page asks the API `GET /admin/me` on each request (`requireAdmin`): a demoted admin or banned account is sent to `/sign-out?denied=1`, which ends the session and shows "That account is not allowed to use the admin app"; the real lock is the API's `AdminGuard`, never this app. The console is a slate sidebar (Overview so far, signed-in email, Sign out) and a content area. Own Vercel project: set `AUTH_SECRET` (same as the API), `API_URL` and `ADMIN_URL` there.
- Reports queue and question log (built 2026-11-05, slice 7, step 3): three admin-only API routes behind `AdminGuard`. `GET /admin/reports?status=OPEN|HANDLED&limit=` lists reports (open ones oldest first, handled newest first) with the words as written, reason, whether it was a question or an answer, `postedAt`, the reporter (null for a report the site raised itself), the author of the reported post (the asker, or the listing's seller for an answer) and the listing, and carries `openCount`; people and listings are looked up by id and show as null or a deleted user when gone, since reports have no foreign keys. `POST /admin/reports/:id/handle` sets `HANDLED` and `handledAt` with a guarded update, so a second click keeps the first time (unknown id 404); the existing clean-up deletes handled reports 30 days after the auction ends. `GET /admin/question-log?questionId=&listingId=&actorId=&limit=` lists `QuestionLog` newest first with the text before an edit. The admin app has Reports (Open / Handled tabs, "Mark handled", a link to that question's log) and Question log pages in the sidebar. Report text is shown as plain text. Not decided yet: whether rating comments get a report button (see REMAINING-WORK); this step covers questions and answers only. Removing the reported post, banning its author and the open-report count in the sidebar are step 4 or later.
- Admin listing removal (decided and built 2026-11-05, slice 7, step 4, first part): an admin takes a live or upcoming auction down with `POST /admin/listings/:id/remove` (`{ reason }`, 1 to 500 characters; `GET /admin/listings/:id` is the summary the admin page reads). **Decision (owner, 2026-11-05): end it early with no sale**, whatever the bids and the reserve, rather than refusing or cancelling and messaging the bidders by hand. It reuses the close path: `AuctionCloseService.closeRemoved` sets `endsAt` (and `startsAt` if still in the future) to now, `removedAt`, `removedById` and `removalReason`, then runs the normal close in the same transaction, which gives outcome `NO_SALE` (`NO_BIDS` when nobody bid), no winner, no order, and one "auction ended" email to every bidder, the high bidder included. The email says a moderator ended it and the bid does not need to be paid. The close statement accepts a row with `removedAt` set even if its end time rounds a fraction of a millisecond ahead of the database clock, and a removal that cannot close throws so the whole transaction rolls back (an early version could leave a listing half removed). The listing leaves the search index at once. Refusals: 404 unknown, 409 `NOT_PUBLISHED` for a draft, 409 `ALREADY_CLOSED` for an ended auction, sold ones included, and 409 `AUCTION_ENDED` when the end time has passed but the close has not run yet (a won auction is never turned into "no sale"; the normal close sells it). Afterwards bids get `NOT_LIVE`, the seller's relist answers 409, and the seller can neither delete the listing nor change its description or photos (409), so it stays as the record, and `GET /listings/:id` carries `removed` (everyone) and `removalReason` (seller only); the listing page shows a banner and hides "list it again". The admin app has a listing page (`/listings/<id>`, reached from a report's listing title) with the summary, a reason box and a confirm before it removes. Not built: an email or bell notice to the seller (they see the banner on the page), hiding a removed listing's content from the public, and removing a sold auction.
- Username squatting: an unverified sign-up holds its username. Decide a cleanup rule for stale unverified accounts before launch.
- Query library for server data: React Query or SWR.
- Payout while sellers have no Stripe onboarding (slice 6), and whether a ban holds back payout (slice 7): decided 2026-11-05, no, a ban does not hold back a payout; payouts run on schedule.
- Account deletion must refuse or anonymise accounts with bids.

**Tooling and hosting**
- Test runner Vitest; package manager Yarn 4 with the `node-modules` linker; Docker Desktop for local Postgres and Redis (2026-10-30).
- API host: Fly.io free trial, no card (owner won't add one). App `auction-api-leke`, region `fra`, `fly deploy -c apps/api/fly.toml --ha=false`. The trial stops the machine 5 minutes after each start and restarts it on the next request (+3-4 s). Consequence: the API can vanish mid-request, so bids use idempotency keys, state is re-checked after failures, the live feed reconnects and resyncs, and nothing lives only in API memory. Web on Vercel, database on Neon (Frankfurt), email on Resend.
- Email: the API sends through Resend directly (`RESEND_API_KEY`, `MAIL_FROM`); production refuses to start without a key; a failed send is 503. Without a verified domain, Resend's sandbox delivers only to the account owner's address.
- Images: a public Vercel Blob store with OIDC and presigned uploads (no long-lived token). The browser uploads via a short-lived URL from `apps/web/app/api/uploads` (own folder, JPG/PNG/WebP, 5 MB). The API accepts only Blob URLs in the seller's own folder and reports removed photos; the web app deletes them, because OIDC works only on Vercel.
- Search host: self-hosted Typesense in Docker Compose, not Cloud (2026-11-02). Cloud's free allowance is one-time and may need a card; the Fly trial machine cannot host it. The code uses a `SearchIndex` interface, so Cloud can be swapped in. Without `TYPESENSE_URL`/`TYPESENSE_API_KEY` the API falls back to `PostgresSearchIndex`, built to match Typesense (whole words plus prefix on the last word, typo tolerance via `fuzzystrmatch`, synonyms, repeated letters cut to two, most-words fallback). `apps/api/test/search-parity.e2e.test.ts` requires equal results from both. Known difference: a swapped letter pair is two typos in Postgres, one in Typesense.
- Search sync is direct from the API, not through an outbox (2026-11-02): listing saves update Typesense; a sweep (start-up and every 5 minutes) and `reindex` repair gaps; searches also filter ended auctions by end time. `BidPlaced` partially updates price, bid count, final-chance and end time, throttled to once per auction per 5 seconds, but extensions go at once.

**Sign-in and accounts**
- An emailed 6-digit code on every sign-in (2026-10-30). Sign-up sends a code and goes to the code page. Later sign-ins: password, then code. A correct code verifies the email. Codes last 10 minutes, are single-use, lock after 5 wrong tries, and a new code voids the old. Signing up again with an unverified email replaces that account's name and password, so nobody can block an address they don't own; a verified email gives 409. After a code is sent, another for the same account answers 429 `CODE_COOLDOWN` for 30 seconds, checked only after the password is right so it reveals nothing.

**Bidding**
- Bid rules (2026-11-02): the transaction judges live/ended/extension by the database clock (`clock_timestamp()` after the row lock), so several machines agree. Only live auctions take bids. Each bid has an idempotency key unique per bidder: a retry answers 200 `replayed: true`. Refusals carry a `code` (`NOT_FOUND`, `OWN_LISTING`, `NOT_LIVE`, `ALREADY_HIGHEST`, `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`, `TOO_LOW` with `minimumNextBidCents`, `BLOCKED`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `BUSY`). A bid is at most EUR 10,000,000 (`MAX_BID_CENTS`) so the next minimum fits 32 bits. A database failure is 404 or 503 `BUSY` (safe to retry with the same key), never a bare 500. A listing with a bid cannot be removed, and neither a listing nor an account with bids can be deleted (`Restrict`).
- Realtime (2026-11-02): Socket.IO gateway, WebSocket transport only (no sticky sessions needed), Redis adapter when `REDIS_URL` is set. Rooms `auction:<id>` (anyone may watch, max 20 per connection) and `user:<id>` (joined by the server from a verified token only). The browser gets a 5-minute HS256 token from `apps/web/app/api/realtime-token`. Events `bid:placed` and `auction:extended` are sent only after the transaction commits, never for a replay; a failed push never fails the bid. The client ignores older messages and refetches the page after every connect or bid-count gap.
- Bid notifications (2026-11-02): after commit, the outbid bidder gets `notice:outbid` plus a delayed email, and other bidders get `notice:extended` when the end moved. Nothing for a first bid, refusal or replay. Toasts last 5 seconds; the outbid toast's "Bid €minimum" button asks for confirmation first. The extension toast is skipped on that listing's own page.
- Limits and audit (2026-11-02): 10 bids a minute per user and 30 per address (`BID_LIMIT_USER`, `BID_LIMIT_IP`), refused attempts included, in a fixed window in Redis (in-memory without Redis). If Redis is down the attempt is allowed, because the database still decides. Every attempt writes a `BidAudit` row (accepted ones inside the bid transaction); malformed or signed-out requests are not recorded. A replay is answered before limits and does not count. A flood writes one `RATE_LIMITED` row per user per minute. The visitor's address is passed from the web server in `x-viewer-ip` with an HMAC signature (`AUTH_SECRET`); the API trusts it only if valid. `TRUST_PROXY` sets how many proxies Express believes. Audit rows are kept as long as bids.
- Outbid email timing (2026-11-02): emails are queued in `PendingOutbidEmail` and sent by a sweeper (every 30 s), due 3 minutes after the outbid (or at once if the auction ends sooner), so a bidding war collapses into one email or none. Skipped if the person is high bidder again or has the auction page open. At most one per person and auction per 15 minutes. A send leases its row and deletes it only after success, retrying up to 3 times, so a stopped machine delays and never loses an email.
- Reserve price: the amount is visible to everyone (2026-10-30). If not met at close there is no sale and the seller can relist.

**Listings**
- Lifecycle: a seller saves a draft, adds photos, then publishes now or at a start time up to 30 days ahead; the end is fixed at publish. Drafts are private and fully editable. After publishing only description and photos change (one photo minimum). A draft can be deleted, and a published listing can be removed until it has a bid (checked and deleted in one statement). Editing a just-published draft answers 409.

**Contacting the seller (slice 4b, 2026-11-03)**
- Public Q&A only. Any signed-in user except the seller may ask on a published, unended auction (up to 1,000 chars); the seller answers once (up to 2,000). Writers can edit or delete their own post until the auction ends (a seller can still post a first answer after); a seller cannot delete a buyer's question but can take back their own answer. Edits show "(edited)" and send no notification. A warning above the box says to keep conversations on the site. If a user is later deleted, their question shows "<deleted user>" and the stored text must be blanked by delete-account.
- A new question notifies the seller and an answer the asker: live toast, bell entry, and email, all best-effort.
- Each question and answer has like/dislike votes; the asker can't vote on their own question, the seller can't vote.
- Limits: 10 questions and 10 reports a minute per person. Reports (reason: spam, abusive, contact details, other) are saved with the words as written, one per person and post, with no foreign keys so they outlive the post until the admin app handles them (handled ones go after 30 days). A post that seems to contain an email or phone number is published but also saved as a reporter-less `CONTACT_DETAILS` report (`containsContactDetails`).
- Every post, edit and deletion is written to `QuestionLog` (no foreign keys, for the admin app) in the same transaction. Log entries and deleted questions are removed 30 days after the auction ends (`QUESTION_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS`).
- In-app notification list: notifications live 14 days; a bell shows the unread count and the latest 20; clicking one marks it read and opens its listing. A newer notice with the same key replaces the older and marks it unread. Expired rows are hidden at once and deleted hourly.

**Close, payment and delivery (slice 5, 2026-11-04)**
- Build order: close logic, "did not win" emails, orders with the fake provider, Stripe, expiry with strikes, relist, QStash last behind `JobScheduler`. Email retries use leased database rows, not a transactional outbox.
- Closing: `AuctionCloseService` is one `UPDATE` matching only an unclosed auction past its end by the database clock, so duplicates, other machines and the sweep (start-up and every 10 s) change nothing, and a bid holding the row lock keeps it open. It sets `closedAt`, `outcome` (`SOLD`, `NO_SALE` if the top bid is under the reserve, `NO_BIDS`), `winnerId` and `finalPriceCents`, then publishes `AuctionClosed`. The listing page shows "Closing the auction..." until saved.
- In the same transaction, closing owes every non-winning bidder an "auction ended" email (`PendingEndEmail`; when the reserve wasn't met, everyone including the top bidder is told there was no sale), deletes pending outbid emails for that auction, and expires its notifications (except each loser's latest "You've been outbid", kept its normal 14 days). No bids sends nothing.
- Orders: closing a sale creates an `Order` for the winner with amount, fee (5%, half cent rounded up, capped at `PLATFORM_FEE_CAP_CENTS`, default 5000), seller net and `payBy` (48 hours from the database `closedAt`), and owes a pay-now email. Only the order's buyer and seller can see it (others get 404). The winner gets a live "You won" toast (not saved, not replayed). The fake provider's `test-pay` works only when `NODE_ENV` is unset, `development` or `test`.
- Stripe: `StripePaymentProvider` is used when `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` are set, else the fake. Hosted Checkout in EUR, card only. The webhook (`POST /payments/stripe/webhook`, signature checked on raw bytes) marks an order `PAID` only if the session is paid, the order is still waiting, the session is one of its attempts, and amount and currency match. Repeats are safe because the update is guarded. `payout` refuses until sellers can be paid (slice 6). Locally use `stripe listen --forward-to localhost:4000/payments/stripe/webhook`.
- Delivery (step 4b, moved forward from slice 6 at the owner's request; tracking, "mark shipped" and payout stay in slice 6): the buyer enters an address and picks an option at `/orders/:id/shipping`, then pays item plus delivery. Delivery is ours, with no carrier integration: 5% of the item price, min EUR 3, max EUR 50, plus the option's extra (Standard +€0, Express +€6, Tracked and insured +€4), computed by the same `packages/shared/src/shipping.ts` on web and API and re-priced server-side. Delivery is never part of the seller fee or the seller's share.
- Pickup address: sellers keep a default in settings; each listing carries its own, required to publish, copied to the order at close, and correctable by the seller any time (waiting and paid orders take the correction).
- Who sees what: the buyer sees their own address, never the pickup address. The seller sees the pickup address, the option, and only the buyer's city and country, never street, phone, or what the buyer paid in all. The admin app reads the full order.
- Address validation (`shippingAddressSchema`): names and cities allow only letters, spaces, dots, hyphens, apostrophes; street lines refuse line breaks and `@`; postcode must fit the country; phone is international (`+<code><number>`, 7-15 digits).
- Paying exactly once: an order has one open checkout page at a time (the order row is locked while one is opened). A changed total closes the old session. When an order is paid its other sessions are closed or, if also paid, refunded. Any non-matching payment (second session, old total, other currency, expired order) is refunded; a failed refund makes the webhook error so Stripe retries.
- After payment, the same transaction takes an invoice number (`INV-<year>-<six digits>`) and queues a buyer email (receipt PDF attached via `pdfkit`; a receipt, not a tax invoice) and a seller email (carrier will collect, share shown). Orders created before delivery existed can't be paid until the buyer chooses delivery.
- Expiry and runner-up: `OrderExpiryService` gives up on orders still waiting after `payBy` (sweep every 30 s, plus `expire(orderId)`). Each expiry is one guarded transaction: the order becomes `EXPIRED`, the buyer gets a `Strike` (one per order), and the item is offered to the next bidder at their own highest bid if it meets the reserve and they were never offered it and aren't blocked (new `Order`, `round + 1`, fresh 48 hours). If nobody qualifies, `outcome` becomes `NO_SALE`. An item is offered to at most 3 buyers (`MAX_PAYMENT_ROUNDS`), worst case about 6 days for the seller. Before expiring, the service asks the provider about the order's sessions: a taken-but-unreported payment pays the order with no strike; if the provider can't be asked the order waits 5 minutes, and after 24 hours past the deadline it expires anyway (a late payment is refunded). Open checkout pages are closed first; if one can't be closed nothing expires. The runner-up gets the pay-now email worded as an offer and a live toast; the non-payer and seller get emails. The strike check on a bid is read before the row lock.
- Payment reminder (decided and built 2026-11-05, owner's request): when 24 hours are left to pay (`PAYMENT_REMINDER_HOURS`), the buyer gets one reminder that the item will be offered to someone else if the order is not paid (and that a non-payer gets a strike): an email, a bell notice and a live toast. It applies to every order, including a runner-up offer's fresh 48 hours. `PaymentReminderService` (`apps/api/src/realtime`, sweep every 60 s, and `sweep()` waits for a pass in progress) finds orders `AWAITING_PAYMENT` whose `payBy` is less than 24 hours away and still in the future and takes a 5-minute lease on each (`Order.payReminderClaimedAt`, a guarded update, so several machines never both send) and sets `Order.payReminderSentAt` only after the email has gone, so a Fly machine that stops mid-send delays the reminder and never loses it (a crash after the email but before the mark can send it twice), and a restarted machine catches up but never sends after the deadline. If the email fails the lease is given back and the next sweep tries again, with no notice or toast sent in between. Banned buyers are skipped. The bell notice reuses the "you won" key (`won:<listingId>`), so it replaces that entry and shows as unread, and paying or expiry removes it as before; its heading is "Less than 24 hours left to pay" and it links to the order. If the payment window is configured under 24 hours the reminder goes out on the first sweep, worded with the time really left. Not built: a second, closer reminder.
- Strikes never expire; an admin clears them in slice 7. At `MAX_STRIKES` (2) bidding is refused with `BLOCKED` and the listing page shows a note. Existing bids and wins stand.
- Platform fee 5% and payout 3 days after delivery (no early buyer confirmation) are config values.
- Relist: `POST /listings/:id/relist` (seller only) copies a closed `NO_SALE`/`NO_BIDS` listing into a new draft (same details, pickup address and photos) and does not publish. Asking again while that draft exists returns it. Photos shared with the old listing are not reported for deletion while another listing uses them.
- QStash: scheduling sits behind `JobScheduler` (`apps/api/src/jobs`). `QStashJobScheduler` is used when `QSTASH_TOKEN`, both signing keys and `API_PUBLIC_URL` are set; otherwise a no-op, and the sweeps do the work. It schedules the close at publish and at each extension, and order expiry at `payBy`. Messages go 1 second late with a deduplication id of job and time joined by hyphens (QStash refuses colons). Scheduling never throws; the sweep covers failures. `POST /jobs/close/:id` and `/jobs/expire-order/:id` are checked by `QStashSignatureGuard` (current key, then next, 5 s tolerance; 401 if bad, 404 if QStash is off) and run the same idempotent operations as the sweeps. They answer 200 even for unknown ids (no endless retries) and 5xx on failure. QStash's free plan holds messages at most 7 days, so none is set more than 6 days out; a call that finds the job not yet due schedules the next hop. Stale messages after an extension are not cancelled, they just pass the baton. Outbid and ended emails stay as leased database rows, since QStash would add only a dependency. Verified against the real QStash dev server on 2026-11-04. Production on Fly: set the four secrets, `QSTASH_URL=https://qstash-eu-central-1.upstash.io` (EU account), and `API_PUBLIC_URL=https://auction-api-leke.fly.dev` (it must be exactly the address QStash calls, because the signature names it). `fly.toml` keeps one machine with auto-start on. The free plan allows 1,000 messages a day, far above need.
- Shipping (decided and built 2026-11-05, slice 6, step 1): `POST /orders/:id/ship` (seller only, paid order, once; refusals `NOT_SELLER` 403, `NOT_PAID` and `ALREADY_SHIPPED` 409) asks the `ShippingProvider` (`apps/api/src/shipping`, `SHIPPING_PROVIDER` token) for a label and sets `Order.shipmentStatus` `LABEL_CREATED`, `trackingCode`, `shippedAt` in one guarded update, so a double click ships once. `FakeShippingProvider` keeps no state: the status is worked out from the time since the label (`FAKE_CARRIER_IN_TRANSIT_SECONDS` default 60, `FAKE_CARRIER_DELIVERED_SECONDS` default 180), so a stopped Fly machine loses nothing. `ShipmentProgressService` (sweep every 30 s, plus `advance(orderId)`) moves the order forward with guarded updates and queues the buyer's `SHIPPED`, `IN_TRANSIT` and `DELIVERED` emails (`PendingEndEmail`, sent by the existing leased sweep) only if the buyer ticked "Email me tracking updates" on the delivery form (`Order.trackingEmails`, off by default, saved by `PUT /orders/:id/shipping`, shown only to the buyer; decided 2026-11-05); everyone else just sees the status on the order page; the seller gets no shipping emails; a parcel found already delivered gets the delivered email only. Both sides see the status, tracking code and times on the order page; the seller gets a "Mark as shipped" button. It does not go through `JobScheduler` (the sweep is the only trigger) and shipping updates are emails only for now, not bell notifications. The payout is step 2.
- Seller payout (decided and built 2026-11-05, slice 6, step 2): **Stripe Connect in test mode**, by separate charges and transfers (the buyer pays the platform; the seller's share is moved later). Sellers get an Accounts v2 `recipient` account with the Express dashboard and the platform owning fees and losses, created on the first "Set up payouts" click (`User.payoutAccountId`; `POST /me/payout-account/onboarding` returns Stripe's hosted onboarding page, `GET /me/payout-account` says `NOT_STARTED`, `PENDING` or `READY`, asked of Stripe live; settings page row and a prompt on the seller's order page). `PaymentProvider` gained `createPayoutAccount`, `createOnboardingLink`, `isPayoutAccountReady` and `payout` (returns the transfer id); the fake marks an account ready when its onboarding link is requested. `PayoutService` (sweep every 60 s, plus `release(orderId)`) pays `sellerNetCents` (price less fee, never delivery money) for a `PAID` order whose parcel was `DELIVERED` at least `PAYOUT_DELAY_DAYS` (3) ago, if the seller's account is ready, by a Transfer tied to the buyer's charge (`source_transaction`, `transfer_group` `order_<id>`, idempotency key `payout_<orderId>`); the `Payout` row (unique per order) is written after, so a crash in between is repaired by the next run getting the same transfer back. **No early release by buyer confirmation** (owner's decision, 2026-11-05). A failed payout is retried after 10 minutes. The seller gets a `PAYOUT` email; their order page shows Payout status (`NOT_DELIVERED`, `SCHEDULED`, `WAITING_FOR_ACCOUNT`, `PAID`) and when it is due; buyers never see it. **Limits found in the docs:** Stripe supports separate charges and transfers only in a list of regions that does not include Kosovo, Albania or North Macedonia, and platform and seller must be in the same region apart from US, Canada, UK, EEA and Switzerland, so test accounts are created in `STRIPE_CONNECT_COUNTRY` (default `de`) and real payouts to the reference countries would need another `PaymentProvider`; Stripe's docs say the onboarding `return_url` must be HTTPS even in tests, but a Playwright run on 2026-11-05 showed the Stripe test API accepted `http://localhost:3000` and opened the hosted onboarding page, so local onboarding works (the hosted page has a captcha, so it cannot be driven by a script). The platform must also have enabled Connect and accepted the loss-liability acknowledgement in the Dashboard (Platform profile), because Express with separate charges needs the platform to be the losses collector; and Stripe refuses `stripe_transfers` in the platform's own region unless the account also requests `merchant` `card_payments`, so both are requested (the account is never used to take payments). The transfer is tied to the buyer's charge (`source_transaction`) only when that charge settled in EUR; otherwise it is paid from the available balance. The owner's sandbox platform is a US account that settles everything in USD, so a EUR transfer could not be funded there (a USD transfer to the ready test seller account worked), and the full EUR payout is unproven until the platform is in a region that settles EUR; the code and the provider-level tests cover it. Checked against the real Stripe test API on 2026-11-05: creating the account, reading its (not yet ready) state and creating the onboarding link all worked. Not built: webhooks for account updates (the account is asked about live instead), an email nudging a seller without an account, and holding a payout for a banned seller (slice 7).
- Watchlist (decided and built 2026-11-05, slice 6, step 3): a signed-in user can watch a live or upcoming auction (`PUT /listings/:id/watch`, `DELETE` to stop; both idempotent and answer `{ watching, watcherCount }`; refusals: the seller 403 `OWN_LISTING`, an ended auction 409 `NOT_WATCHABLE`, a draft or unknown listing 404). `Watch` is one row per user and listing, removed with either. `GET /listings/:id` carries `watcherCount` for everyone and `watching` for a signed-in viewer; the listing page shows a Watch / Watching button with the count (everyone else sees the count). `GET /watchlist` lists up to 200 watched auctions as listing cards: live first (ending soonest), then upcoming (starting soonest), then ended (latest first). The `/watchlist` page (menu item "Watchlist") shows them with All / Live / Upcoming / Ended filters and a "Stop watching" button on each card, so the user picks which to open or drop. **Email:** one "Ending soon" email per watcher and auction, 24 hours before the end (owner's choice, 2026-11-05): watching writes a `PendingEndEmail` row of kind `ENDING_SOON` due at `endsAt` minus 24 hours (removed with the watch), sent by the existing leased sweep. An auction already inside its last 24 hours owes none. At send time nothing goes out if the person stopped watching, the auction has ended or closed, or they are the high bidder (or the seller). The due time is not moved by anti-sniping extensions (at most 24 hours of extra time, and the email is only a reminder). Reminders are sent at most 25 per sweep and after all other waiting mail, so a crowd watching one auction cannot hold up pay-now or payment emails; the text says how long is really left when a reminder goes out late. A person can watch at most 200 auctions (409 `WATCHLIST_FULL`; watching the same one again is fine). No bell notification for it.
- Seller ratings and profiles (decided and built 2026-11-05, slice 6, step 4): after an order is `DELIVERED`, its buyer rates the seller once, 1 to 5 stars and an optional comment of up to 500 characters (`POST /orders/:id/rating`; refusals `NOT_BUYER` 403, `NOT_DELIVERED` and `ALREADY_RATED` 409, bad input 400). One `Rating` per order (unique order id, so two tabs or a double click rate once), checked 1 to 5 by the database, and it cannot be changed or removed by the buyer. Both sides see it on the order page ("Your rating" / "Buyer's rating"); the buyer gets a rating form there once delivered. `GET /users/:username` (public) is the seller profile at `/sellers/[username]`: username, member since, average (one decimal) and count, the latest 20 ratings with the rater's username and the item's title, and what the seller has on sale now (up to 12 live or upcoming listings); never a real name or email. The seller name on the listing page and the buyer's order page links to it. The watchlist and the profile share one listing-card mapping (`apps/api/src/listings/listing-card.ts`). Not built: replying to a rating, reporting or moderating rating comments (slice 7 reads reports), a rating summary on listing cards, and seeded ratings.
- Clearing notifications (added 2026-11-05, at the owner's request): each notification in the bell has an X that removes it (`DELETE /notifications/:id`), and "Clear all" empties the inbox (`DELETE /notifications`); both delete the rows and answer the new unread count, only ever the caller's own. A newer notice about the same thing brings it back as a new one. If the server refuses, the list is loaded again so the bell shows the truth.
- Win notification (added 2026-11-05, owner's request, for consistency with the "you won: pay now" email): when a sale closes, or the item is offered to the next bidder, the buyer's bell gets a saved `WON` notification keyed `won:<listingId>` ("You won the auction" or "The item is yours to pay for", with "Pay now") that links straight to `/orders/<id>`, alongside the live toast and the email. It is removed when the order is paid or expires, and can be dismissed like any other. The live toast is unchanged.

## 8. Build order and done-criteria

Thin vertical slices. Each works end to end, with tests passing, before the next starts.

1. **Monorepo, DB, auth.** Turborepo builds everything; Docker Compose runs Postgres and Redis; migrations run; sign-up, verify and sign-in work; NestJS verifies the Auth.js JWT; CI runs lint, typecheck, tests.
2. **Listings and images.** Create, edit and publish a listing with Blob images; categories seeded; listing pages render.
3. **Search.** Live and upcoming listings sync to the index; typo tolerance, filters, sorting; ended auctions drop out.
4. **Bidding and realtime.** Bids obey the minimum rule; live WebSocket updates; anti-sniping; outbid toast and email; 50-concurrent-bids test has exactly one winner; rate limits and audit log.
4b. **Contacting the seller.** Public Q&A with live notification and email, no self-questions, rate limits and reports.
5. **Auction close and payment.** QStash closes idempotently; winner gets the pay-now email, chooses delivery, pays in Stripe test mode, and gets a confirmation with PDF invoice; 48-hour expiry triggers the runner-up offer and strikes; fee applied; every outbid bidder gets one "you did not win" email.
6. **Shipping, payout, watchlist, ratings.** Seller marks a paid order shipped; the fake carrier advances it on a timer with shipping emails; payout is released 3 days after delivery (timer only) through `PaymentProvider.payout`; watchlist and "ending soon" emails; seller profiles with buyer ratings.
7. **Admin.** `apps/admin` (own theme and Vercel project, no public sign-up, `ADMIN`-guarded routes) supports moderation, the reports queue, clearing strikes, reading `QuestionLog` and an order with both addresses; a demoted admin or banned user loses access at once; the admin theme is chosen. Build order (decided 2026-11-05): (1) role and ban freshness in the API, `AdminGuard` and `GET /admin/me`; (2) the admin app shell, admin-only sign-in and the slate/indigo theme; (3) the reports queue and `QuestionLog`; (4) moderation: remove listings, ban and unban users, clear strikes; (5) reading an order with both addresses. Split from slice 6 on 2026-11-04.
